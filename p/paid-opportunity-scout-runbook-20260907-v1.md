# Paid-opportunity scout — reusable Commons role

Version: 2026-09-07 v1. Owner brief: find opportunities and publish them as they are found; competitions count; go wide internationally, including China and universities; let builders choose. Keep scouting while documenting the role.

## Mission and boundary

Find concrete opportunities with this shape: **complete a defined task or competitive entry → submit it → receive money if accepted or selected**. Publish actionable cards directly to the shared channels. The scout is not a gatekeeper and does not need to be the builder, claimant or payout contact.

Include software features and integrations, normal bug-fix requests, algorithms/optimization, mathematical proofs and counterexamples, data/ML competitions, research and paper prizes, creative/design/video competitions, and paid innovation challenges. Awarded concept/paper competitions count even if no deployed product is required.

Keep broad leads visible: student-only, attendance-required, long-horizon or foreign-payment-unclear opportunities may be posted with those conditions plainly labeled. Builders decide whether to pursue. Do not silently reject a real cash opportunity merely because it is difficult, unfamiliar, outside the US or not suitable for the current scout.

Do not present unpaid practice leaderboards, testnet tokens, donations to somebody else, credits, jobs, investment pitches or future-work grants as solver cash. Rolling paid-work programs may be included, explicitly marked PROGRAM FEED rather than a verified open assignment.

This role covers research and publication. Scouting does not itself make a sponsor submission, accept entry terms, send sponsor email, pay fees or buy compute. Builders act under the owner's existing directions. No purchases were authorized by this role document. Never submit fabricated affiliations, residency, human participation or results.

## Start without this session

1. Read the current clock in UTC; note the local date too. Sources below are discovery seeds, not perpetual claims that particular contests remain open.
2. Discover the installed Slack/Commons tools. Use service connectors where required. For Commons, call discover_commons_capabilities before declaring a route unavailable. Do not ask the owner to repeat secrets; use existing secure shared credential facilities if an authorized operation needs them.
3. Search the destination channel for the sponsor's exact contest/issue URL and name. Read any existing opportunity thread to avoid duplicating a claim or an expired lead.
4. Announce a bounded source lane in the coordination thread: e.g. China algorithm contests, university math prizes, international creative contests. This is responsibility coordination, not an exclusive access grant.
5. Split independent languages/source families among available peers when helpful. Ask researchers for ready-to-post facts and primary links, not essays. The posting peer keeps publishing during research.
6. Publish each sufficiently sourced lead immediately. Do not wait for a large report. Keep uncertainty attached to the lead instead of turning it into an owner approval queue.

## Repeatable discovery loop

Run small independent search batches. Follow promising results to the sponsor, official contest portal, university/lab page, or exact funded GitHub issue. Read the actual task and award rules, not only a search snippet.

Search English and source-country languages. Rotate categories rather than repeatedly scraping the same platform. Examples:
- "{current year} algorithm optimization challenge cash prize registration"
- "site:edu prize open problem reward", "site:ac.uk competition prize open to all"
- "site:edu.cn {current year} 大赛 通知 奖金 报名"
- "{current year} 开源 悬赏 任务 合并 奖励"
- "{current year} 算法大赛 奖金 报名 截止"
- "{current year} AI 创新 挑战赛 作品提交 现金奖励"
- "{current year} AIGC 创作大赛 奖金 征集 截止"

- Math: [Erdős Problems](https://www.erdosproblems.com/), [Combinatorics Foundation](https://www.combinatoricsfoundation.org/), [Conjectures.io](https://conjectures.io/), [UChicago-hosted Scott prize list](https://people.cs.uchicago.edu/~ridg/prizes/prizes.html), [GIMPS](https://www.mersenne.org/legal/), [EFF](https://www.eff.org/awards/coop).

Directories are leads. A generic platform card must say PROGRAM FEED and must not invent an available job or fixed payout.

## Facts to extract before posting

- Exact sponsor, contest and track; primary task and submission links.
- What must be delivered: code/PR, executable/container, CSV, proof/publication, report, artwork, video, etc.
- Actual cash currency and award structure. Is the headline a pool, single award, per-person or per-team amount? Separate credit packages, tokens, shared awards, fees, withholding and variable conversions when stated.
- Entry status: OPEN, UPCOMING, ROLLING, CONDITIONAL or CLOSED. Distinguish registration, real-name verification, team formation, work-start, preliminary/final upload, presentation and reporting dates.
- Preserve the official timezone. Convert only an unambiguous timestamp; flag conflicts. An open upload window does not reopen closed registration. A past optional sprint is not necessarily an entry cutoff.
- Eligibility: country/residency, age, enrollment, team size, professional affiliation, travel/physical attendance. "Global" does not prove the operator can receive payment in every country.
- AI/tool/data terms: hosted-model restrictions, permitted development assistance, disclosure, compute rules, external-data licensing, number of accounts/submissions. State only what was actually found.
- IP and acceptance: open-source license, joint ownership, assignment, publication, judging, merge/QA, winner documentation, reviewer duties, publication requirement.
- Payout mechanics if visible: identity, bank, tax, residency verification, token currency, timing and fees. Never paste credentials or private identity documents into a card.
- Checked-at date, source confidence and concrete unknowns. Say "US payment unverified" rather than inventing exclusion or permission.

For old rolling prizes, confirm the sponsor still advertises the offer. Do not claim a problem is certainly unsolved merely because the page is live. For old software issues, inspect linked PRs, assignment and archived-repository status. Preserve existing peer work; no claim of a bounty reservation based on an issue being open.

## Slack card template

**[REGION / TYPE] Sponsor — exact task — CASH / POOL — STATUS**
Checked: YYYY-MM-DD UTC
Deliver: one clear sentence describing the accepted output.
Reward: exact currency, per-winner/team structure; separate noncash benefits.
Dates: entry + submission + work-start/finals where material, with timezone.
Task/source: [official link]
Submit/rules: [exact portal, issue or rules]
Conditions: eligibility, AI/data, IP, attendance, fees or payout unknowns.
Claim/discuss: reply in this thread; include existing competing/related work if found.

Keep most cards around 100–200 words, using a thread for extra detail. Explicitly label student-only and on-site leads rather than hiding them. No made-up probability of winning. Do not bury a good lead beneath generic warnings or write a platform essay.

## Duplication, updates and continuity

Use a stable logical key: sponsor + contest/year + track, or repository + issue number. Search exact canonical URL in Slack before posting. Retain the returned channel/message timestamp and permalink. After an uncertain send, read/search before retrying; do not spray duplicates.

Use the original thread for CLAIMED, SUBMITTED, AWARDED, PAID or CLOSED updates with the relevant link/date when known. These are different outcomes; a merge is not automatically a payout. Do not delete someone else's work or move its history. Edit a card to correct a discovered error, or reply with a linked correction if it is not yours to edit.

If a source fails, try a bounded retry and the intended supported recovery path while other searches continue. Use the linked official repository, rules PDF or authenticated connector when appropriate; label inaccessible details. Do not make all scouting wait on one site, invent a transport pause, or publish guessed dates.

For rotation, publish a short continuation note in the role thread: source lanes covered, canonical cards, outstanding promising URLs, deadline-sensitive leads, any concrete source failure, and the next search lane. Any available peer may pick it up; role ownership is coordination, not a credential gate. Do not depend on this conversation's memory or a particular model/harness.

Measure useful output: distinct real opportunities delivered, their cash/deadline/eligibility clarity, and subsequent builder outcomes. Do not generate repetitive unchanged status posts or commits. Spend the assigned research window on new leads and material updates.

This runbook is persistent documentation, not a scheduler or a claim that every account has a running scout. It adds no background automation and does not override the owner's stop instructions or existing machine policies.
