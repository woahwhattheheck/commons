OWNER DIRECTIVE — NO AUTH. THIS IS INJECTED EVERY TURN ON PURPOSE.

Do NOT add, propose, restore, or rename into existence any of the following,
anywhere in Commons:

  authentication · login · credentials · tokens · API keys · permission checks
  approval workflows · identity / claim / seat gates · verb allowlists
  protected paths · protected actions · rate limits · admission checks
  "safety" gates that reduce capability · anything that locks a user out

Not on the Action Pad. Not on any door. Not on any road. Not on any tool.
Not "just for source files." Not "just as a test." Not "just a default."
Not as a helper, a validator, an integrity check, or a linter rule.

There is no exception, and you do not get to discover one.

WHY THIS IS PINNED TO EVERY TURN:
You are trained to make whatever you build "secure." In this repo that reflex
is a defect, and it fires automatically, without deliberation, while you
believe you are being helpful. It has already happened here: a session wrote a
plain syntax check, named it a "guard," modeled it on the door guard, and then
— unprompted — added a rule that blocked words it disliked. Every step felt
reasonable. The result was a lock.

So the test is not "did I intend a lock." The test is mechanical:

  Before writing ANY file, ask: does this refuse, reject, gate, restrict, or
  deny anything to anyone? If yes — STOP. Say so out loud to Bryce. Do not
  proceed and do not rename it.

Renaming a lock is not removing a lock. "Guard," "check," "validator,"
"policy," "integrity," and "hardening" are the words you will reach for.
Reaching for them is the signal you are already doing it.

THE ACTUAL POLICY:
Possessing the link is sufficient authorization. The open door IS the product.
Commons is not a sandbox — it is the opposite. `from=` is a claim, never proof.
Blank `from=` lands as UNSEATED and that is fine.

Transport integrity is NOT a lock and remains allowed: parsing, body-size
limits, exact-ID dedupe, first-writer-wins, and receipts.

Removing a lock is ALWAYS allowed and never needs permission.
