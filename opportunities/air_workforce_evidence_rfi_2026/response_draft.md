## 4. Overview of the AI-enabled workforce innovation — 300 words max

We propose an evidence-bound AI career-navigation assistant studied alongside a workforce-delivery partner. The assistant would help participants prepare questions, interpret approved labor-market or training information, plan next actions, and surface uncertainty to a human coach. It would not make eligibility, hiring, benefits, placement, or adverse decisions. The technical design emphasizes source/provenance binding, deterministic event receipts, safe retries, model/policy versioning, human override, and explicit escalation so the research can distinguish useful assistance from automation failures. Candidate AIR focus areas are **Career Navigation and Job Placement** and **Personalized Support**, with Skill Training and Development included only if the delivery partner supplies a real training context. [OWNER_INPUT_REQUIRED: actual implementation boundary, models/tools/data, partner role, and maturity.] The research concept is to test not merely whether participants like an AI tool, but which tasks, populations, conditions, and safeguards produce measurable benefit or harm.

## 5. Workforce challenge — 200 words max

Workers and learners often face fragmented information, repeated handoffs, and limited coaching time while navigating training and job opportunities. A useful AI system might reduce search and preparation burden, but a poorly governed one can amplify stale information, obscure uncertainty, create inconsistent guidance, or shift work onto participants and coaches. [PARTNER_INPUT_REQUIRED: concrete workforce setting, affected population, existing service journey, baseline friction, and why this problem matters there.] The proposed study would center a real delivery problem rather than deploy AI for its own sake.

## 6. Key learning questions — 200 words max

Which navigation tasks benefit from AI assistance versus human coaching or static resources? How do usefulness, error, escalation, abandonment, and downstream milestones differ across participant groups and contexts? Which safeguards—source grounding, abstention, human review, structured escalation, and constrained tools—reduce consequential errors without eliminating utility? How do participants and coaches adapt their behavior over time? Which implementation conditions explain variation in outcomes? [OWNER/PARTNER_INPUT_REQUIRED: prioritize questions that match actual service decisions and available measures.]

## 8. Technical approach, models, or data sources — 300 words max

The proposed architecture separates participant/service policy from the AI workflow. The workforce partner would control participant eligibility, service rules, approved content/data sources, referrals, and consequential decisions. The technical layer would expose bounded model/tool capabilities with explicit provenance, model/prompt/policy versioning, human override, abstention/escalation, request idempotency, and tamper-evident event receipts. Evaluation telemetry would use opaque participant/session identifiers and minimize retained data. [OWNER_INPUT_REQUIRED: actual model providers, retrieval sources, hosting, integrations, security controls, and data flows.] [PARTNER_INPUT_REQUIRED: actual service data, consent authority, retention rules, and outcome feeds.] No production participant data should be transferred merely to make the RFI appear mature.

## 11. Responsible AI and safeguards — 200 words max

The study should pre-specify fairness and error analyses across relevant participant groups; disclose when AI is involved and where information comes from; minimize participant data and separate identity from research telemetry; version models, prompts, tools, and policies; keep humans authoritative for consequential decisions; and define abstention, escalation, incident review, and change-control paths. Worker/learner and coach voice should shape unacceptable-error classes, explanations, accessibility needs, and burden measures. [OWNER/PARTNER_INPUT_REQUIRED: bind these principles to actual controls, consent/privacy governance, retention, security, accessibility, language access, and responsible owners.]

## 12. Opportunities for partnership with AIR — 150 words max

AIR could serve as an independent research/evaluation partner: refine learning questions and a pre-analysis plan; select a feasible randomized, stepped-wedge, quasi-experimental, or formative design; validate measurement and subgroup analysis; study implementation/context; and translate interim and final evidence into field-facing guidance. The delivery partner would own workforce services and participant relationships; the technical team would support instrumented AI workflows and reproducibility. [OWNER/PARTNER_INPUT_REQUIRED: confirm the proposed roles, study feasibility, data-governance path, and what the team is genuinely prepared to learn or change based on evidence.]
