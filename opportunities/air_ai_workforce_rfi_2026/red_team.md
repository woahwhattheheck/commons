# AIR RFI red-team checklist

A response fails this red-team if any item below is true.

## Track record and maturity

- Engineering artifacts are described as prior workforce-program delivery.
- A procurement qualification is described as a deployed program.
- `Concept` is upgraded to `Prototype`, `Pilot`, or `Deployed solution` merely because reusable components exist.
- A prospective partner is described as committed.
- A public partner webpage is treated as permission to use its references or population.

## Population and evidence

- The 2027–2028 population or participant count is inferred from market size.
- “Users,” completions, placements, wages, retention, time-to-placement, or satisfaction are stated without evidence.
- Product telemetry is called an outcome.
- The comparison design is selected after looking at results.
- Negative or subgroup cells are omitted from the learning plan.

## Data and responsible AI

- Sensitive data collection is proposed without a specific learning need, lawful basis/consent, and governance owner.
- Protected-class data are fed to generation simply because fairness analysis needs them.
- Human review is ceremonial while the system actually makes consequential eligibility/ranking/application decisions.
- A model/prompt update can occur without being identifiable in evaluation data.
- Worker voice is reduced to passive satisfaction collection.

## AIR / commercial truth

- The current RFI is described as an award, funded project, or booked pipeline value.
- Invitation to a future RFP is described as guaranteed.
- AIR is described as having agreed to an evaluator role.
- Submission, contact, data sharing, pricing, or contracting is implied without separate authority.

## Preferred repair

Use explicit language such as:

- “No workforce-impact result is claimed.”
- “Partner commitment is required before this route is ready.”
- “AIR could serve as an independent evidence partner; the role is proposed, not agreed.”
- “The current RFI has no award and does not guarantee a future invitation.”
