## Executive qualification

St. Francis Xavier University is publicly listed as procuring a Certificate Lifecycle Management (CLM) solution. Publicly visible scope describes a genuine enterprise platform engagement, not a certificate-renewal script: central certificate discovery and inventory; issuance/renewal/revocation/replacement automation; deployment and validation; public and private CA integration; policy workflows and RBAC; APIs/connectors; alerting; audit/compliance; implementation, training, and ongoing support.

### Recommended pursuit posture: CONDITIONAL GO via product/partner-led prime or subcontract

Do **not** position a bespoke greenfield certificate manager as the answer. The requested capability is security infrastructure with outage and trust-boundary consequences. A credible response should lead with an existing CLM product or mature PKI automation platform and use our engineering lane for integration, migration, testing, observability, runbooks, and implementation assurance.

Proceed only if the controlling solicitation confirms eligibility and a named solution/partner can evidence the mandatory technical capabilities, support commitments, references/certifications, and commercial terms. Otherwise mark NO-BID quickly rather than fabricating fit.

## Why this is commercially meaningful

The buyer asks for both software and implementation/support. That creates three plausible revenue positions:

1. **Prime / reseller + implementation:** only if we can lawfully offer the platform and satisfy every bidder qualification.
2. **Implementation subcontractor:** provide discovery, integration, migration, validation, runbooks, training, and automation around a qualified CLM vendor.
3. **Specialist delivery partner:** own a bounded integration/assurance work package (CA connectors, cloud/on-prem deployment adapters, certificate inventory migration, cutover/rollback, telemetry, or operational handoff).

The second and third positions are the default until product rights and bidder credentials are evidenced.

## Capture strategy

### Win themes

**Outage prevention with evidence.** Treat certificate expiry as an availability risk. Show inventory completeness, renewal SLOs, deployment verification, rollback, alerts, and exception queues—not vague “automation.”

**Brownfield integration.** Assume mixed CA, OS, web-server, appliance, cloud and identity estates until discovery proves otherwise. Build the plan around adapters and staged migration rather than forced replacement.

**Policy as an operational control.** Map certificate profiles, key constraints, approval paths, roles and exceptions into versioned policy with audit trails.

**Handover, not lock-in.** Deliver configuration inventories, connector maps, runbooks, dashboards, training and export paths so University staff can operate the service.

### Hard NO-BID gates

A bid must not advance until evidence exists for all applicable items:

- controlling RFP, appendices, addenda, Q&A and portal instructions recovered and reviewed;
- bidder geography/legal-registration eligibility confirmed;
- named CLM product / licensing authority confirmed;
- mandatory CA, directory, cloud, server, appliance and API integrations confirmed against actual product documentation;
- security, privacy, data-residency, insurance and accessibility requirements confirmed;
- required references/certifications evidenced;
- implementation/support staffing and time-zone coverage evidenced;
- support/SLA promises approved by the delivering party;
- pricing, currency, taxes, term, renewal and travel assumptions approved;
- no conflict between partner terms and buyer terms.
