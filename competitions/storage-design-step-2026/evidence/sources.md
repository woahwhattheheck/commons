## Chemistry / architecture anchors

4. Liu, X.; Li, T.; Yuan, Z.; Li, X. **Low-cost all-iron flow battery with high performance towards long-duration energy storage.**
   *Journal of Energy Chemistry* 73 (2022), 445–451. DOI: 10.1016/j.jechem.2022.06.041
   https://www.jenergychem.com/EN/10.1016/j.jechem.2022.06.041
   Published system uses ferri/ferrocyanide with ferric/ferrous gluconate in alkaline aqueous electrolyte.
   The paper reports >99% coulombic efficiency, ~83% energy efficiency at 80 mA/cm², >950 cycles,
   12/16/20 h cycling demonstrations, and a projected $76.11/kWh 10 h / 9.9 kW system.
   **Boundary:** these are third-party experimental/projection results, not results from this entry.

5. Gong, K. et al. **All-Soluble All-Iron Aqueous Redox-Flow Battery.**
   *ACS Energy Letters* (2016). DOI: 10.1021/acsenergylett.6b00049
   https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsenergylett.6b00049
   Establishes prior art for all-soluble alkaline all-iron coordination chemistry and identifies ligand
   crossover / membrane resistance as key challenges. This prevents us from claiming the chemistry itself as new.

6. **All-iron redox flow battery in flow-through and flow-over set-ups: the critical role of cell configuration.**
   *Energy Advances* (2024). DOI: 10.1039/D4YA00179F
   https://doi.org/10.1039/D4YA00179F
   Demonstrates that cell configuration materially changes power, efficiency, utilization, and fade in
   all-soluble all-iron systems; supports treating stack architecture as an engineering variable.

7. Zhang, S. et al. **All-soluble all-iron aqueous redox flow batteries: Towards sustainable energy storage.**
   *Energy Storage Materials* 75 (2025), 104004. DOI: 10.1016/j.ensm.2025.104004
   https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ensm.2025.104004
   Review identifies continuing challenges in solubility, electrolyte stability, electrode reactivity,
   membrane selectivity, and capacity decay.

8. **Current status of ferro-/ferricyanide for redox flow batteries.**
   *Current Opinion in Electrochemistry* 48 (2024), 101581. DOI: 10.1016/j.coelec.2024.101581
   https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coelec.2024.101581
   Notes ferro/ferricyanide stability is sensitive to pH, air, and light; lifetime/EHS controls must be designed,
   not assumed.

9. **Membrane contamination-driven sulfonate structuring for enhanced stability in all-iron redox flow batteries.**
   (2025).
   https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405829725002260
   Reinforces membrane/ligand-crossover sensitivity and shows active membrane engineering remains a live research area.
