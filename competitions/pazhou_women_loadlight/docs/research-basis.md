# Research basis and claim discipline

LoadLight is built around a measurable problem, but the prototype avoids turning population-level gender research into a claim about any individual household.

## Evidence

### OECD — unpaid care and domestic work

OECD Employment Outlook 2025 reports that women in OECD countries spend about four hours per day in unpaid care and domestic work versus about two hours for men. It also notes that time-use surveys undercount “on call” and cognitive domestic work.

Source: OECD, *Employment Outlook 2025*, Box 2.6, “Gender gaps in unpaid work.”
https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-employment-outlook-2025_194a947b-en/full-report/component-6.html

### Cognitive household labor

Aviv et al. studied 322 mothers of young children and separated cognitive/planning labor from physical execution across 30 household tasks. Mothers reported responsibility for 72.57% of cognitive labor versus 27.43% for partners, compared with 63.64% of physical domestic labor. The study also found associations between cognitive-labor share and multiple wellbeing outcomes.

Source: Aviv E, Waizman Y, Kim E, et al. *Cognitive household labor: gender disparities and consequences for maternal mental health and wellbeing.* Archives of Women's Mental Health 28, 5–14 (2025).
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00737-024-01490-w

### U.S. time-use data

BLS reports that on an average day in 2025 women spent 2.4 hours on household activities versus 1.6 hours for men.

Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, American Time Use Survey 2025 Results.
https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2026/time-spent-in-personal-care-activities-averaged-9-8-hours-per-day-in-2025.htm

### Cross-national cognitive labor

Haupt and Gelbgiser analyze survey data from 10 European countries and find a high share of cognitive labor is associated with family–work conflict for women, while emphasizing the need for better measurement of cognitive work.

Source: Haupt A, Gelbgiser D. *The gendered division of cognitive household labor, mental load, and family–work conflict in European countries.* European Societies 26(3), 828–854 (2024).
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/14616696.2023.2271963

## What this evidence does not prove

It does not prove:

- that every woman carries more household work than every man;
- that LoadLight reduces stress, burnout, conflict, or depression;
- that the stage weights are clinically or psychometrically validated;
- that a handoff suggestion is fair or should be accepted;
- that the prototype has real-user product-market fit.

Those are future validation questions.

## Planned validation

A serious pilot should separately measure:

1. classification precision/recall against participant-confirmed stage labels;
2. percentage of hidden cognitive tasks newly made visible;
3. handoff acceptance and completion, not just suggestions generated;
4. user-reported coordination time before/after;
5. false positives and perceived intrusiveness;
6. whether the product helps the person carrying more cognitive work *without* creating new administrative burden.
