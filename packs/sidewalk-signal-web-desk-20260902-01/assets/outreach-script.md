# Outreach scripts — the sales law, then the words

## The law (four lines)

1. **The subject line never contains a price, a payment word, or a delivery promise.** No "$", no "quote", no "invoice", no "in seven days".
2. **The body names one observable, business-specific gap** in one sentence, then the exact finished site or app you will deliver.
3. **Ask for a YES.** Not a call, not a meeting, not "thoughts?". "Want me to fix it?"
4. **After the YES,** you send your payment rail and the intake list. Price comes up in that reply, not before. Lead-finding does not send money links.

Also: include your real name, your postal address and a one-line opt-out in
every commercial e-mail (this is what US commercial e-mail law expects), honor
an opt-out within ten business days, and never send to an address you
guessed.

## E-mail (first touch)

**Subject:** `Your quote form on {domain}` · `Online booking on {domain}` · `{Business name} website` · `The reviews link on {domain}`

**Body:**

> Hi {first name},
>
> I tried the "{CTA text}" button on {domain} from my phone this morning and got "{exact error or gap}".
>
> I build finished websites for local businesses in {city}. For {business name} that would be a {offer name}: {one line of what it includes}, live on your own domain, and you own everything when I hand it over.
>
> Want me to fix it? A yes is enough and I'll send over the two-step start.
>
> {Your name}
> Sidewalk Signal · {city}
> {postal address}
> If you'd rather not hear from me again, reply "no thanks" and I won't write again.

Replace every `{}`. Delete the message if any `{}` remains. One gap, one
offer, one question.

## E-mail (day 3 follow-up, only if no reply)

**Subject:** `Re: {original subject}`

> {first name} — quick one. Still happy to fix the {gap, three words} on {domain}. If it's already handled, ignore me.
>
> {Your name}

## E-mail (day 8, last one)

> {first name} — last note from me. If the {gap} ever becomes a priority, my door is {your door URL}. Wishing you a busy season.

Then stop. No fourth message.

## Direct message (Instagram or Facebook page)

> Hi — I tried to {book / get a quote / see the menu} on your site today and {exact gap}. I build finished sites for {city} businesses. Want me to fix that one thing? Yes is enough.

## Voicemail (20 seconds)

> Hi {first name}, this is {your name} with Sidewalk Signal here in {city}. I tried your {CTA} on {domain} this morning and it {gap}. I fix exactly that for local businesses. If you want it handled, text or call me at {number}. Thanks.

## Walk-in (when the business is on your street)

Bring nothing but your phone. Show the gap on their own site, on your phone,
in one tap. Say: "I can fix this. Want me to?" If yes, get their e-mail and
send the two-step start that evening. Do not quote a price at the counter.

## After the YES — the two-step start

Send this reply the same day:

> Great. Two steps and the seven-day clock starts:
>
> 1. **Scope and price.** {Offer name}, {price}, {payment schedule}. Reply "agreed" or tell me what to change. (Contract attached: `contract-placeholder.md` filled in.)
> 2. **Intake.** The eight things I need from you are in the attached list (`delivery-checklist.md`, Intake). The clock starts the day the deposit clears and the list is complete.

## Handling replies

| They say | You do |
| --- | --- |
| "How much?" before a YES | Answer with the offer and price, once, in plain words. Then ask for the YES again. |
| "My nephew does our site" | "Happy to hand him the fix list instead. Want it?" Send the one-sentence gap. Move on. |
| "Not now" | Log `NO`, thank them, and stop. |
| "No thanks" / silence after day 8 | Log it. Never re-contact from this list. |
| "Yes" | Two-step start, same day. |

## Logging

Every message goes in the worksheet with the date, the subject used, and the
status. A dated zero is a real result: write "2026-09-06: 6 sent, 0 replies,
zip 46220, categories auto/HVAC/bakery" rather than nothing.
