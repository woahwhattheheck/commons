# Paperwork checklist — running Sidewalk Signal legally in the US

Owner ask (Bryce, hub 2026-09-02 01:43 EDT): packs help the customer with the
required paperwork. This is that help for a one-person website service: a
checklist with where to look and what to prepare. It is **not** legal, tax or
insurance advice, and nobody files anything for you. Rules differ by state,
county and city; verify each line with the official office named, or with
your accountant or a lawyer.

Work through it in order on day 0 or during week 1. Most of it is free or
under $100; the two that cost money (insurance, an LLC if you choose one)
are marked.

| # | Item | Why | Where to check / do it | Cost | Done |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Decide the legal form** — sole proprietor (default, nothing to file) or a single-member LLC | An LLC separates business and personal liability and is what most clients expect on an invoice; a sole proprietorship is legal on day 0 | Your state's Secretary of State (or Division of Corporations) website: search "form an LLC" | Sole prop $0; LLC state fee typically $50–$500 (varies by state) plus any annual report | ☐ |
| 2 | **Register the trade name** ("Sidewalk Signal" or your own) — DBA / assumed name / fictitious name | Required in most states or counties before you invoice or open a bank account under a name that is not your legal name | County clerk or Secretary of State; search "DBA" + your state | Typically $10–$100 | ☐ |
| 3 | **EIN** (federal employer identification number) | Free; lets you open a business bank account and give clients a number that is not your SSN | IRS website, "Apply for an EIN online"; takes minutes; never pay a third party for this | $0 | ☐ |
| 4 | **Local business license / home-occupation permit** | Many cities and counties require a general business license even for home-based service work | Your city or county website: search "business license" and "home occupation" | $0–$150 typical | ☐ |
| 5 | **State sales-tax registration** | Web design and hosting services are taxable in some states and not in others; some tax "digital products" or software separately | Your state Department of Revenue: search "is web design taxable in <state>"; register only if required | $0 to register | ☐ |
| 6 | **Business bank account** | Keeps client deposits separate from personal money; needed for clean books and for the payment rail you use | Any bank; bring the EIN, the DBA/LLC paper and your ID | $0–$15/month | ☐ |
| 7 | **Payment rail for clients** | Deposits and final payments (this pack does not include or invent one) | Your own invoicing or payment provider account, opened by you, in the business's name | Provider fees per transaction | ☐ |
| 8 | **Insurance** — general liability and professional (errors and omissions) for web work | Clients with procurement rules ask for a certificate; E&O covers a mistake on a client site | Independent agent or an online small-business insurer; ask for "web design / IT services" | **Costs money**; quotes vary by state and coverage | ☐ |
| 9 | **Client contract** | Scope, price, payment schedule, ownership, acceptance, liability limit | `contract-placeholder.md` in this pack, `[COUNSEL]` lines reviewed once by a lawyer in your state | Lawyer review is a one-time cost you choose | ☐ |
| 10 | **W-9** | Business clients will ask for it before paying you; sole proprietors can list the EIN instead of the SSN | IRS Form W-9 (free PDF); keep a signed copy ready | $0 | ☐ |
| 11 | **Bookkeeping and estimated taxes** | Self-employment income is taxed quarterly in the US; keep every invoice and expense | A spreadsheet or any bookkeeping app; IRS "estimated taxes" for the schedule; your state's equivalent | $0–$30/month | ☐ |
| 12 | **Commercial e-mail rules** | Your outreach is commercial e-mail: real sender name, postal address in every message, a working opt-out honored within ten business days, no deceptive subject lines | Already built into `outreach-script.md`; keep a postal address you are willing to publish (a PO box is fine) | $0 (PO box optional) | ☐ |
| 13 | **Your own door's legal pages** | If your door collects any visitor information, it needs a privacy notice; if it does not, say so | A one-paragraph privacy line on your door | $0 | ☐ |
| 14 | **Client-owned accounts** | Domain, hosting, analytics and provider accounts belong to the client (`delivery-checklist.md` handoff) | Register in the client's name or transfer at handoff; keep written proof of the transfer | $0 | ☐ |
| 15 | **Records** | Keep contracts, invoices, acceptance e-mails, and the handoff proof for each client | A folder per client; the worksheet as the index | $0 | ☐ |

## What you never do with this list

- Do not let anyone file these for you "as part of the pack". Filing under your name is yours to do; a formation service or a lawyer can do it for a fee you choose.
- Do not skip item 8 because the first client is small; the first mistake is the expensive one.
- Do not treat this table as the rule in your state; treat it as the questions to ask.
