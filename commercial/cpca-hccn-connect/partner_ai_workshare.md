## Workstream A — AI governance readiness

### Goal

Turn a health center's AI activity from ad-hoc tool adoption into a reviewable operating model with explicit decision rights, data boundaries, human oversight, change control, monitoring, and incident ownership.

### TJLabs deliverables

1. **AI use / vendor inventory schema** — use owner, workflow, affected users, data classes, vendor/model dependency, decision impact, automation authority, human checkpoint, and current evidence.
2. **Risk-tier rubric** — a practical triage mechanism for administrative, operational, clinical-adjacent, and higher-risk uses. The rubric supports governance; it does not make legal or clinical determinations.
3. **Decision-rights matrix** — who can propose, review, approve, pause, change, and retire a use.
4. **Control / evidence map** — each required control tied to an observable artifact rather than a prose assertion.
5. **Change and incident path** — vendor/model/version changes, unexpected output, data-path change, user escalation, rollback owner, and retained evidence.
6. **Prioritized governance backlog** — owners, dependencies, acceptance evidence, and stop conditions.

### Acceptance evidence

- every inventoried use has an owner and stated data boundary;
- each risk tier has an explicit review / approval path;
- each active or proposed AI use has at least one measurable outcome and at least one stop / rollback condition;
- vendor/model changes have an owner and evidence-retention path;
- unresolved claims remain gaps, not automatically converted to compliance.

## Workstream B — AI vendor evaluation

### Goal

Give the prime / health center a repeatable way to separate vendor claims from decision-grade evidence and compare products against the health center's actual constraints.

### TJLabs deliverables

1. **Evaluation matrix** covering use-case fit, integration dependencies, security/privacy evidence supplied by the vendor, data retention/use, model-training/improvement terms, subprocessors, human oversight, auditability, accessibility, support, change notification, export/exit, and operational failure modes.
2. **Evidence ledger** distinguishing vendor assertion, contract term, product documentation, observed behavior, independent test, and unresolved claim.
3. **Scenario-based evaluation harness** for non-production/synthetic or otherwise authorized test data, with expected behavior and failure criteria declared before scoring.
4. **Weighted decision record** that records assumptions and sensitivity rather than hiding them behind a single unexplained score.
5. **Gap / procurement-question log** for facts that must be resolved before purchase or implementation.

### Acceptance evidence

- scoring criteria are frozen before candidate scores are finalized;
- every material score has a source/evidence pointer or is marked unknown;
- missing security/privacy evidence does not silently become pass;
- the deliverable identifies switch/exit risks and model/vendor change dependencies;
- the prime retains the legal, procurement, security and compliance decision.

## Workstream C — AI use-case education and readiness

### Goal

Help participating teams distinguish attractive demos from feasible, measurable health-center use cases without turning a training session into unsupported implementation claims.

### TJLabs deliverables

1. **Use-case canvas** — problem, affected workflow, user, current baseline, data sensitivity, decision impact, failure cost, human checkpoint, expected benefit, and measurable pilot outcome.
2. **Risk / value exercise** — structured comparison of expected benefit, implementation friction, data exposure, reversibility, explainability/audit need, and operational burden.
3. **Pilot hypothesis template** — measurable success threshold, minimum evidence, test cohort/data boundary, owner, rollback trigger, and decision date.
4. **Reusable facilitator materials** — worksheets, worked synthetic examples, and facilitator notes that the prime can adapt to its own training program.

### Service-type boundary

TJLabs can provide the technical exercises and artifacts. **Group Training must not be proposed as a TJLabs qualification merely because these materials exist.** If CPCA Group Training is selected, the applicant must separately prove the buyer's required curriculum-based, multi-organization instruction experience, learning objectives, facilitation, participant engagement and evaluation history.

## Workstream D — AI implementation assurance

### Goal

Provide a bounded technical assurance layer when the prime is implementing an approved AI use: observable acceptance criteria, failure testing, change detection, rollback evidence, and human authority checks.

### TJLabs deliverables

1. **Implementation acceptance plan** — inputs, expected outputs/behaviors, non-goals, dependency versions, human checkpoints, data boundary, and rollback owner.
2. **Failure-injection test plan** — malformed/edge input, unavailable dependency, latency/timeout, permission failure, stale data, model/vendor change, and unsafe automation-path checks as relevant to the system.
3. **Evidence capture** — deterministic test IDs, timestamped results, environment/version identifiers, source/config hashes where available, and explicit unknowns.
4. **Change-control hook** — conditions requiring re-evaluation before a new model, vendor version, prompt/system policy, data source, connector, or automation authority is promoted.
5. **Handoff pack** — open risks, failed tests, accepted exceptions, owner, and rollback/runbook pointers.

### Boundary

AI Implementation is offered only if the prime's selected CPCA route and actual comparable-engagement evidence support it. This sheet does not make TJLabs or the prime qualified for that subdomain by itself.

## Standard engagement sequence after marketplace qualification

This is an internal delivery pattern, not a CPCA commitment.

1. **SOW shaping:** prime + participating health center define the actual outcome, data boundary, service type, deliverables, hours/budget, timeline and approval path.
2. **Evidence intake:** collect only the minimum documents / system facts needed to execute the selected workstream; classify unknowns explicitly.
3. **Baseline:** record current workflow, current controls / failure paths, and measurable starting state.
4. **Build / evaluate:** execute the selected TJLabs workstream with inspectable intermediate artifacts.
5. **Acceptance:** test against agreed evidence-based criteria; unresolved failures stay visible.
6. **Handoff:** deliver reusable artifacts, decision record, owner map, risks, and next actions.
7. **Monthly evidence:** prime can use hours + milestone evidence to support CPCA's required monthly approval/invoicing process; TJLabs never claims buyer approval or revenue before it occurs.

## Fast pre-deadline teaming decision

A prospective prime does **not** need to disclose private client material merely to decide whether a teaming conversation is real. Before either party spends submission time, confirm these six binary facts:

1. **Prime intent:** the organization actually intends to pursue CPCA HCCN Connect in the 2026-09-18 review window.
2. **Applicant gate:** the prime believes it can substantiate CPCA's applicant-level licensing/insurance, health-center experience, capacity and reference requirements.
3. **AI route:** the prime expects to select at least one AI subdomain/service type for which it can support the required comparable-engagement evidence.

5. **Authority:** an authorized prime representative can approve TJLabs as a proposed subcontractor/support provider and define what, if any, prime evidence TJLabs may reference internally for qualification assembly.
6. **Submission ownership:** the prime owns the final application, rates, references, resumes, attestations, signatures, and CPCA submission unless a written agreement says otherwise.

If any of 1–4 is `NO`, close the lane cleanly. If 5–6 are unknown, keep the lane at discussion-only and do not promote `TEAMING_READY`.

## Commercial boundary

No subcontract rate, CPCA rate, margin, hours, minimum volume, award probability, or revenue is committed here. CPCA states that marketplace approval does not guarantee referrals or a minimum volume of work. Commercial terms must be approved by the relevant human owner / prime and then reflected in the actual SOW or subcontract.

The economic proposition to a prime is capacity and delivery leverage: TJLabs takes a bounded technical AI work package with explicit acceptance evidence, while the prime retains the healthcare relationship, applicant qualifications, CPCA contract surface, and domain accountability.

## Data / safety boundary

- Default to synthetic, de-identified, public, or otherwise expressly authorized test data during evaluation and assurance.
- Do not place PHI, PII, credentials, private reference contacts, confidential health-center material, or protected buyer/partner evidence in public Git.
