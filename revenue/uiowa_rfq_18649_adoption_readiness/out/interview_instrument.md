# Organizational AI adoption readiness - interview instrument

**Instrument id:** `UIOWA-077-AIR-v1`

> DRAFT INSTRUMENT CONTENT. These prompts are a starting point for operational interviews. Nothing here is a University finding or a measured University figure.

> **Recording rule.** Responses are recorded at TEAM granularity with a contributor COUNT only. No individual identity is collected, stored, or attributed. This instrument assesses organizational capability and must never be used to rate a person.

Record each indicator as `ABSENT`, `EMERGING` or `ESTABLISHED` **with the evidence you actually looked at**, or leave it unobserved. An unobserved indicator is not an `ABSENT` finding - it resolves to `UNKNOWN` and becomes an evidence request.

Evidence kinds: `artifact`, `system_record`, `interview_statement`. An indicator supported only by an interview statement cannot reach `ESTABLISHED`; recollection is not a record.

## AI literacy

*Whether the team shares a written, consultable understanding of where these tools belong and where they do not.*

### LIT-1

**Ask:** Where has the team written down what these tools are used for here, and what they are not used for?

**Ask to see:** The written statement or guidance page, with the date it was last reviewed.

**Why it matters:** A shared written boundary is what lets two people on the same team answer a borderline request the same way.

### LIT-2

**Ask:** When someone is unsure whether a task is appropriate for an assistant, what do they consult before deciding?

**Ask to see:** The page, checklist, or thread they name.

**Why it matters:** Distinguishes a consultable resource from an individual habit that leaves with the person.

### LIT-3

**Ask:** How does the team describe what these tools get wrong on the work you actually do?

**Ask to see:** Any written note of known failure modes for the tasks currently in use.

**Why it matters:** Naming specific failure modes is the difference between literacy and enthusiasm.

## Practical training

*Whether capability was built on the team's own recurring work, and whether it survives staff turnover.*

### TRN-1

**Ask:** What hands-on practice has the team had on its own recurring tasks, as opposed to a general awareness session?

**Ask to see:** Training record or session notes naming the tasks practised.

**Why it matters:** General awareness rarely transfers to the team's actual queue.

### TRN-2

**Ask:** How does someone joining the team learn the assisted parts of the workflow?

**Ask to see:** Onboarding material that covers the assisted step.

**Why it matters:** Capability that exists only in the current staff is a dependency, not a capability.

## Workflow fit

*Whether assistance is placed at identified points in real procedures, including where it was deliberately removed.*

### FIT-1

**Ask:** Which recurring workflows currently include an assisted step, and at which point in the procedure?

**Ask to see:** The procedure document showing the step.

**Why it matters:** Placement in a written procedure is what makes the practice repeatable by someone else.

### FIT-2

**Ask:** Where was an assisted step tried and then narrowed or removed?

**Ask to see:** The change note, ticket, or decision record.

**Why it matters:** A recorded retreat is strong evidence of real evaluation; its absence can mean nothing was ever checked.

### FIT-3

**Ask:** Which steps are explicitly excluded from assistance, and on what basis?

**Ask to see:** The written exclusion and its stated reason.

**Why it matters:** Exclusions show the boundary is operational rather than aspirational.

## Available support

*Whether there is a reachable, non-singular place to take a problem with an assisted workflow.*

### SUP-1

**Ask:** Who does the team ask when an assisted workflow behaves unexpectedly?

**Ask to see:** The named contact, queue, or routing rule.

**Why it matters:** Separates a support path from an informally helpful colleague.

### SUP-2

**Ask:** What happens to that question when the usual contact is unavailable?

**Ask to see:** The escalation path, if it is written down.

**Why it matters:** Single-person support is the most common hidden dependency in this area.

## Knowledge sharing

*Whether something one person learns becomes reusable by the team, and whether it is kept correct.*

### SHR-1

**Ask:** Where does a useful approach that one person worked out get recorded so others can use it?

**Ask to see:** The register, page, or channel, with its actual entries.

**Why it matters:** Sharing that happens only in conversation does not survive a staffing change.

### SHR-2

**Ask:** How is that shared material reviewed or corrected when it stops being right?

**Ask to see:** Review or last-checked dates on entries.

**Why it matters:** An unreviewed register becomes a source of confidently wrong practice.

## Capacity to evaluate new approaches

*Whether the team can tell a genuinely better result from a faster-looking one, and can reverse a decision.*

### EVL-1

**Ask:** Describe the last new approach the team trialled and how it decided whether to keep it.

**Ask to see:** The evaluation note or decision record.

**Why it matters:** Trialling without a recorded decision leaves the team unable to explain why it works the way it does.

### EVL-2

**Ask:** What would the team check to distinguish a genuinely better result from one that merely arrives faster?

**Ask to see:** The acceptance check or known-answer set, if any exists.

**Why it matters:** Without an acceptance check, speed is the only thing anyone can actually observe.

### EVL-3

**Ask:** Who can stop an approach the team has already adopted, and on what evidence?

**Ask to see:** The written decision owner or reversal criterion.

**Why it matters:** An adoption nobody can reverse is a commitment, not an evaluation.
