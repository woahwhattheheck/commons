# UIOWA-024 — Interview and artifact sampling field guide

**Status: proposed assessment method; not an appointment, an approved engagement plan, or a finding about the University.** All people, systems, artifacts and observations in the companion worked case are fictional. This guide does not certify the RFQ's wording or replace the prime's review of the controlling solicitation.

This is the method and editable matrix requested by the [original UIOWA-024 work order](https://tokenjunkielabs.slack.com/archives/C0C2M1K2V4P/p1789824405808939). Its planning inputs are six to eight interviewees per service group, supporting artifacts, and a six-to-eight-week delivery window. ESS, RIS and IAM are the three service groups used here. Confirm that interpretation, relevant boundaries and permitted access with the engagement lead before applying it to real work.

**Start here:** complete the [editable coverage matrix](coverage-matrix.md), then use the [worked selection and limitation cases](worked-selection.md) to check the proposed plan. The method works as an ordinary facilitated worksheet. It does not depend on an unpublished optimizer or create meetings.

## 1. What the sample is intended to explain

The sample supports a constructive explanation of how selected work is performed, how practices differ across documented contexts, where accounts agree or conflict, and which practical improvements deserve validation. It is a **purposeful, coverage-oriented sample**, not a probability sample of employees, applications, incidents, or changes.

Six to eight participants is a planning target for each group, not a statistical sample-size calculation. More participants do not by themselves make the sample representative. A group of eight developers on the same stack can leave operations, support, security, seasonal work and failed changes unexamined. A smaller group with those perspectives may expose more useful questions, while still falling below the target.

There are five distinct units. Keep their counts separate:

| Unit | Identifier and count | What the count does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Person | Stable pseudonymous participant ID; deduplicate across all services | Independent statistical observations or confirmed availability |
| Person within a group | Distinct participant ID plus group | A different person when someone supports two groups |
| Interview bundle | Proposed session ID and actual attendance record | One meeting per person, or a calendar invitation |
| Artifact | Source locator, version, window and access status | Complete population coverage or authenticity merely from a label |
| Supported statement | Exact proposition, evidence references and limitations | A maturity score or evidence of every other practice |

A session agenda covers questions. Attendance establishes that particular people participated. Reviewed evidence may support a particular statement. None of these states automatically produces the next one.

## 2. Establish a frame before choosing participants

Ask the service lead for a **role-and-work inventory**, not a list of only the most enthusiastic demonstrators. Record the inventory's source, date, scope, omissions and nomination route. Until those are known, mark frame coverage `UNKNOWN`. Do not interpret an absent row as an absent role, system or practice.

For each group, propose context-specific requirements in three separate dimensions:

- **Role perspectives:** service accountability; implementation/integration; verification; operations/recovery; consumer support; security/identity. These are conversation perspectives, not mandatory job titles. A person may credibly cover several, but record the basis rather than assuming that a title confers coverage.
- **Technical contexts:** the actual important stacks, integrations, managed services and data paths identified in the frame. The example's three stack tags per group are fictional placeholders, not a University inventory.
- **Delivery patterns:** routine changes, time-critical or seasonal work, exceptional recovery, access lifecycle, project-bound work, or other patterns the frame identifies. Replace the example tags instead of treating them as a universal checklist.

Also maintain a separate agenda across `software`, `security`, `deployment`, and `ai`. These four subject areas do not substitute for the three sampling dimensions. A conversation about deployment is not proof that the operations role or an incident-response pattern was included.

Use stable pseudonymous IDs in the planning matrix. Keep real names, contact details and sensitive affiliations only in the approved engagement workspace with the minimum access needed. Do not publish real interview rosters, private artifacts, credentials or University records into the public demonstration repository.

## 3. Select grouped conversations deliberately

Prepare candidate bundles around connected workflows: for example, service intent and verification; integration and operations; security and exceptional changes. A bundle can include two or three complementary perspectives, with a separate confidential route for dissent or sensitive matters. A joint conversation is not a reason to suppress disagreement or force a manager and subordinate to give one account.

Use this reproducible selection process. It is a documented decision procedure, not a claim of mathematically optimal selection:

1. Freeze the current frame version and the required perspective/context/pattern sets. Record who proposed those sets and what remains unknown.
2. Mark each person's **proposed**, **availability-unchecked**, **declined**, **unavailable**, or **participated** state. Only observed participation can populate actual attendance. A proposed plan may use unchecked candidates but must display that condition.
3. Select a first bundle for each group before concentrating all initial capacity in one group. Prioritize a missing critical perspective over repeating the same role and stack merely to reach a count.
4. Add bundles that cover the documented missing dimensions. Count each person once per group and once globally, even when they attend more than one bundle. Keep session appearances as a separate workload measure.
5. Check the six-to-eight target, assessor capacity, shared-person overlap and the evidence window. Leave conflicting choices visible. Record why an alternative was not selected; do not silently discard it.
6. Give the engagement lead the specific gaps and alternatives. Proposed times, substitutions, extra participants, expanded access and scope changes require ordinary human agreement. This worksheet neither checks a live calendar nor commits anyone to attend.

A candidate's stated experience is only a planning assertion. Confirm relevant firsthand experience during the conversation and revise the coverage record when a claimed perspective is not actually represented. Preserve the original nomination and the correction; do not rewrite the earlier frame to make the choice appear perfect.

**Tie handling:** where two candidates cover the same declared gaps, record a transparent reason such as contrasting work context, an independently nominated perspective, or less burden on a shared participant. Use a stable ID ordering only as an explicitly arbitrary planning convenience, not as a claim about who is more qualified. Unresolved ties can remain alternatives for the lead.

## 4. Pair the interviews with artifacts

For each intended statement or workflow, request a bounded artifact chain rather than an unrestricted system export. A useful chain can be request/decision → implementation/change → verification → deployment/operation → exception/follow-up. A chain can be incomplete; record where it stops.

Start with a declared ordinary-work example and a contrasting example, such as a failed, delayed, emergency, legacy, cross-team or boundary case. This is a proposed search strategy, not a fixed artifact quota. Keep nominated showcase examples labeled as such. Where the frame permits, ask whether another route can identify a contrasting example. Do not claim random selection unless an actual sampling frame and random procedure were used and retained.

For every artifact request record: ID, group, topic, nominated-by route, intended question, source locator, version, event window, export coverage, access status, review state, and any conflicting reference. An interview assertion that an artifact exists is not a retained artifact. A supplied file with a convenient title is not proof of complete coverage or of its source's authenticity.

`software`, `security`, `deployment`, and `ai` are routing labels for the assessment. Not every service necessarily has observed AI use. A missing AI inventory remains an evidence request; a statement of no use remains that person's account until the scope and supporting basis are established. Do not manufacture an AI-readiness finding simply to fill the matrix.

## 5. Treat a limited evidence window explicitly

Before selection, record the intended event window and the actual available window for each source. Distinguish the time an event occurred, the time a record was created, and the time an export was obtained. An export created after the window may contain in-window events; a later event cannot be relabeled as in-window simply because its export is current.

When an export covers only part of the intended period, retain the covered portion, name the missing interval, and narrow statements accordingly. Do not extrapolate a quiet fortnight to a peak season, or treat the absence of incidents in an incomplete export as proof of no incidents. Where a major seasonal or unusual workflow lies outside the window, propose a separately labeled historical contrast or a future follow-up; neither silently changes the original period.

A deleted, inaccessible, unexported, redacted, unavailable, or not-yet-requested record is not the same as a complete source explicitly showing zero matching records. Both the source's declared coverage and the collection process matter. A declared complete export may still need corroboration; it is not self-authenticating.

Use three statement styles:

| Situation | Appropriate statement pattern | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reviewed bounded chain | “In the selected examples and stated window, the retained records show …” | “The team always …” |
| Conflicting accounts | “The interview account and artifact reference disagree about …; resolution is open.” | Selecting the more convenient version without explanation |
| Missing or partial source | “The requested evidence was unavailable/partial, so this question remains unresolved.” | “The practice does not exist.” |

## 6. Additional-sample and stop decisions

Expand to answer a **specific unresolved question**, not merely to accumulate interviews. Record each trigger in the matrix with affected scope, next evidence, proposed owner role, and a decision state. These are work requests, not automatically dispatched tasks.

| Trigger | First response | Expansion condition | Closeout if access/time is unavailable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Group has fewer than six distinct participants | Identify which required perspectives are missing | Add a missing perspective within agreed capacity; document any exception to eight | State the count, missing perspectives and interpretation limit |
| Six to eight participants but a critical role/stack/pattern is absent | Rebalance or substitute a proposed bundle | Add targeted participation only when the unanswered question warrants it | Preserve undercoverage despite the headcount target being met |
| Nominations all come through one manager or showcase route | Ask for a contrasting nomination route and record its scope | Seek an alternate account with relevant firsthand work | Label nomination concentration; do not call the sample unbiased |
| Two sources materially disagree | Preserve both references and formulate the exact disputed proposition | Obtain the event/version/decision record or another relevant account | Publish the conflict as unresolved; do not average incompatible accounts |
| Missing, inaccessible or partial artifacts | Clarify export scope and the reason for missingness | Request a bounded alternate source or different case | Narrow the window or proposition; missingness stays explicit |
| Shared person dominates several groups | Show global identity and repeated appearances | Add a genuinely distinct perspective where needed | Do not claim the repeated account is independent corroboration |
| Material legacy/peak/emergency context lies outside the available window | Label the uncovered context | Add a separately labeled contrast with a justified window | Keep the context unassessed; do not silently extend the main window |
| New evidence changes the interpretation | Retain the old snapshot and exact changed support | Reopen only affected questions and downstream statements | Record what remains unknown and why |

A bounded sampling round can close when each group has an explicit count, every required dimension is either represented or listed as a gap, each material contradiction has a disposition, every used artifact has an interpretable source/window, and remaining limits are carried into the report. “No new themes in the last conversation” is useful process context, not statistical saturation or proof of completeness.

Do not prolong interviews indefinitely to chase perfect coverage. At the agreed evidence freeze, preserve unresolved work with its impact and recommended next step. Only the engagement lead may agree to an extension or scope change.

## 7. Fit within six to eight weeks without making appointments

These are relative phases after an agreed kickoff, not calendar commitments. Neither dates nor attendees are booked by this guide.

| Phase | Six-week path | Eight-week path | Retained output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Frame and access planning | Week 1 | Weeks 1–2 | Scope, frame source/limits, proposed roster, approved request routes |
| Initial grouped conversations and bounded artifacts | Weeks 2–3 | Weeks 3–4 | Attendance distinct from proposals; versioned notes and source/window records |
| Contrasts, missing dimensions and conflicts | Week 4 | Weeks 5–6 | Targeted follow-ups, substitutions, unresolved-question log |
| Interpretation and factual checking | Week 5 | Week 7 | Traceable draft statements, preserved dissent and coverage appendix |
| Readout and carryover | Week 6 | Week 8 | Final limitations, decisions and explicitly unfinished work |

The worked case spreads nine 45-minute bundles over three illustrative weeks with a three-bundle weekly capacity. That is 6.75 hours of session time, 13.5 assessor-hours with two assessors, and 20.25 participant-hours from 27 appearances. These numbers exclude preparation, consent/access checks, analysis, artifact review, follow-ups, travel and reporting. They are fictional workload arithmetic, not a fee or staffing commitment.

## 8. Required reporting and handoff

Every report using this sample should carry: the frame source and limits; requested versus actual participants per group and globally; repeated-person involvement; represented and missing dimensions; planned versus actually discussed topics; requested versus reviewed artifacts; source/event windows; nomination concentration; unresolved contradictions; substitutions and departures from the plan; and the evidence freeze date.

Do not produce a combined “representativeness score,” employee ranking, maturity rating or confidence percentage from these counts. Do not pool three groups into one denominator that conceals a weakly sampled group. Do not attach confidence intervals or population failure-rate claims to these purposeful cases. The sampling worksheet is not a replacement for specialized statistical-method review when a later quantitative claim genuinely needs one.

### Method reference and limits

Baltes and Ralph, *Sampling in Software Engineering Research: A Critical Review and Guidelines*, [version 6, October 20, 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07764v6), distinguish probability and non-probability sampling and discuss appropriate limits on representativeness claims. That supports the distinction used here; it does not validate this fictional roster, prescribe six to eight interviewees, or establish any University assessment result.
