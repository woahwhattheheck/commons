# Law and platform-policy flags for selling and advertising business packs

Seat: SCOUT (Fable 5.1). This is research, not legal advice. Every item marked **OWNER** needs Bryce's decision or his counsel's; this seat flags and cites, it does not rule.

## 1. FTC Business Opportunity Rule, 16 CFR Part 437 (current text)

Source: [govinfo CFR 2025 Title 16 Part 437](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title16-vol1/xml/CFR-2025-title16-vol1-part437.xml).

A "business opportunity" exists when (1) a seller solicits entry into a new business, (2) the buyer makes a required payment, and (3) the seller represents it will do any one of:
- provide **locations** for equipment, displays, vending machines, or similar;
- provide **outlets, accounts, or customers**, including internet outlets or accounts;
- **buy back** goods or services the purchaser makes.

If covered: a disclosure document at least **seven calendar days** before contract or payment (§437.2); any **earnings claim** ("any oral, written, or visual representation... that conveys, expressly or by implication, a specific level or range of actual or potential sales, or gross or net income or profits") requires a reasonable basis, written substantiation available on request, and an earnings statement showing the share of purchasers who achieved it (§437.4). Franchises under Part 436 are exempt except very small ones.

**What this means for packs as currently specified.** A pack of assets + instructions + week-1 calendar, with **no** promise of customers, locations or buy-back, is not a "business opportunity" under the current rule. The moment a pack says "we give you your first customers", "leads included", "we place your machines", or the like, it is covered. **OWNER:** decide per tier whether packs ever include customers/leads (the $10,000 card is where the temptation is, because Apollo credits exist). If yes, plan for 437 compliance; if no, keep the language to "the method to find customers".

**Copy rule for everyone, regardless of coverage:** no earnings figures, ranges, or implied income in ads, doors, or pack files. Prices and time budgets are fine ("$40 per bin-out", "two hours Saturday"). "Make $200 this weekend" is an earnings claim.

## 2. FTC proposed changes (January 2025 NPRM) — pending

Source: [FTC press release 2025-01](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-proposes-rule-changes-new-rule-deter-deceptive-earnings-claims-multilevel-marketers-money-making).

The FTC proposed expanding the Business Opportunity Rule to cover "money-making opportunities, such as business coaching and investment opportunities, which claim to assist consumers in building a business or otherwise earning income", with prohibitions on material misrepresentations and a written-substantiation duty for earnings claims; a separate proposed Earnings Claim Rule for MLMs; and an ANPRM on waiting periods and earnings-data disclosure. Vote was 3–2 with two dissents; 60-day comment period from Federal Register publication. **Status as of this research: proposed, not final** (this seat did not find a final rule; **OWNER/counsel** to confirm current status before scaling spend). If adopted as proposed, "a package that helps you build a business" is squarely inside scope even without customer promises. The copy rule above (no earnings claims, ever) is the cheapest insurance either way.

## 3. X Ads policies that touch this product

Sources read directly: [Deceptive and Fraudulent Content](https://business.x.com/en/help/ads-policies/ads-content-policies/deceptive-and-fraudulent-content), [Gambling content](https://business.x.com/en/help/ads-policies/ads-content-policies/gambling-content), [Financial services](https://business.x.com/en/help/ads-policies/ads-content-policies/financial-services).

- **Prohibited outright** under Deceptive and Fraudulent Content: clickbait CTAs; misleading claims; omitting pricing/payment terms/expenses the customer will incur; "get rich quick", MLM, pyramid schemes; **"unrealistic claims, results within a specific period, or health or economic opportunities"** (the "Impractical Outcomes" clause); cloaked landing pages; non-functional CTAs.
  - Consequence: ads must state the price, must not promise results in a period, and must not read as an "economic opportunity". "Start a business this weekend" is borderline; "a kit to start a weekend yard-help route, $100" is a product description.
- **Gambling content** (restricted; prior authorization from X required in permitted countries): the policy covers **lotteries**, **scratch games**, "games or apps played for money", and "free-to-play games that result in a prize of value". The **U.S.** section (read in full 2026-09-02) lists lottery ads, "Pay-to-Play (P2P) online/offline non-casino game ads" and digital lottery courier ads as "permitted with restrictions": advertiser must be US-domiciled, ads must carry appropriate disclaimers, comply with all X ads policies, and "may be subject to additional requirements at X's sole discretion"; authorization "starts with being in touch with an X account team". Canada explicitly names "lottery and sweepstakes ads permitted with restrictions" with prior authorization. 
  - Consequence for "mystery nuts": any ad copy about a "chance to draw" or "you might get" a valuable bonus risks classification as a sweepstakes/lottery promotion by automated review, and an "Offering cash gifts" prohibition sits next door. **Keep the nuts off the ads.** Describe the guaranteed pack in the ad; if the bonus is mentioned at all, it belongs on the door in the owner's exact framing ("a fun, generous gesture", not a prize), with no odds and no scarcity, as the factory law already says.
- **Financial services** (restricted, certification required): "investment opportunities, and offers to increase wealth" are in the permitted-with-restrictions list. Do not describe a pack as an investment, a return, or wealth-building; describe it as a product.
- **Sensitive Category Verification** for restricted categories takes 10–20 business days and requires business registration documents `(secondary)` ([auditsocials](https://www.auditsocials.com/platforms/x-ads-policy)). Better to write copy that never enters a restricted category.

## 4. The "mystery nuts" mechanic under US lottery law — OWNER

Factory law (Bryce, ts `1788323180.640899`): each price pool mixes in rare, valuable ideas; buyer has a chance to draw "the nuts"; framed as generosity, not lottery, not gambling; no invented odds, no fake scarcity.

US law applies a three-element test regardless of framing: **prize + chance + consideration** = lottery unless a defense applies ([brandmovers legal guide](https://www.brandmovers.com/complete-legal-guide-digital-promotions-and-contests), [track360 mystery box compliance](https://track360.io/blog/mystery-box-gambling-or-shopping-operator-compliance-guide)). Mystery-box operators survive by (a) guaranteeing every purchase delivers value at least equal to price so the "prize" is a bonus on a completed sale, (b) offering a no-purchase alternate method of entry, and (c) disclosing odds; Washington and Idaho are cited as blocking probability-draw consumer mystery boxes outright ([tech-insider legality by state](https://tech-insider.org/mystery-boxes/are-mystery-boxes-legal-in-the-usa/)) `(secondary)`.

Marketing consequence (this seat's lane): the guaranteed pack is the product in every ad; the bonus is never the reason to buy in copy. Legal consequence (**OWNER/counsel**): whether the nuts mechanic needs a guaranteed-value statement, state exclusions, or odds disclosure before the first sale. The factory law's "no invented odds" is compatible with Bryce publishing real odds if counsel wants them.

## 5. Payments and refunds — OWNER

- Card networks act at ~0.65% chargeback rate; platforms like Gumroad pause payouts above 3% and force refund policies above 1% disputes `(secondary)` ([swell Gumroad pricing](https://www.swell.is/content/gumroad-pricing), [fungies refund policy guide](https://fungies.io/digital-product-refund-policy-guide-2026/)). Stripe Payment Links are the rail here; Stripe's own dispute thresholds apply.
- A $20 refund costs the fee twice; low-ticket digital goods run higher friendly-fraud rates. A written refund policy on the door (digital goods, delivered instantly, terms stated before purchase) lowers disputes and is also what X's "omitting vital information on pricing, payment terms" clause expects.
- The unique-instance law helps here too: "you received a named instance with these files" is a strong dispute response.

## 6. Naming — the word "franchise"

Do not call any pack a franchise or use franchise vocabulary in ads. The FTC Franchise Rule (16 CFR 436) is a separate disclosure regime; comparisons to franchise fees are fine when sourced (e.g., Card My Yard $10,350), but the product is a package, not a franchise.

## 7. Checklist before the first ad (owner reads, this seat drafted)

1. Ad and door state the price and what is included. No earnings, no results-in-a-period, no "opportunity" wording.
2. No lottery/draw/win/prize/chance language in ads. Bonus, if mentioned, only on the door in the owner's framing.
3. "Unique / nobody else gets this" only when `host/business_pack_unique.py` returns `UNIQUE` for the instance.
4. Refund policy written and linked on the door.
5. Decide whether any pack includes customers/leads (437 coverage). Default: no.
6. Confirm NPRM status with counsel before scaling past test budgets.
7. X Pixel thank-you page exists (see X_ADS_BRIEF §5).
8. The advertising handle is verified (Verified Organizations or Premium), posts are public, and the bio URL is live and ungated; X will not accept campaigns otherwise ([eligibility](https://business.x.com/en/help/ads-policies/campaign-considerations/about-eligibility-for-x-ads)).
