# Messaging angle — "become your own boss" (owner direction, 2026-09-02 ~01:40 EDT)

Owner, in-session to SCOUT, verbatim: *"become your own boss angle. become business owner for this price, 'your own employee and employer' 'level up' 'we did most of the work for you!' that kind of stuff."*

This file tests that angle against each buyer card and against the ad-policy and FTC constraints already on file, and drafts candidate lines **for Bryce to use**. Marketing execution stays his. Nothing here is shipped copy; every line is checked, not chosen.

## 1. Why the angle is right for these buyers

- It sells **identity and permission**, which is what every card says the buyer is actually paying for (BUYER_TIERS §0: the removal of "what do I start" plus permission to begin this week). "Business owner" is the identity; "we did most of the work" is the permission.
- It is **truthful in a way the competing market is not**: the $17 prebuilt-store trade promises income; this angle promises ownership and work already done. Ownership is verifiable (the instance has a brand and a domain); "most of the work" is verifiable (the asset list and the week-1 calendar exist).
- It is the franchise industry's own vocabulary ("be your own boss, but not by yourself" is the standard franchise pitch), which is exactly the comparison the $1,000 and $10,000 buyers are making, and it lets our door say the thing franchises cannot: no royalties, you own the brand.
- **"Your own employee and employer"** is the most useful phrase in the set. It says out loud that the buyer will do the work. That is the anti-"passive income" positioning, it pre-empts the refund/complaint pattern of the DFY market, and it is the phrase least likely to trip any platform's income-claims review.

## 2. Policy and law check on each phrase

| phrase | status | why | how to keep it clean |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Become your own boss" | **clear** | identity claim, not an earnings claim; FTC 437 defines earnings claims by income/sales figures, and platform rules target "unrealistic results" and "economic opportunities" promised, not status | never pair it with a dollar figure, a timeline, or "quit your job" in the same line; "your own boss on Saturdays" is truthful for the route pack |
| "Become a business owner for $100" | **clear with two conditions** | (1) X and TikTok both reject ads "omitting vital information on pricing, payment terms, or expenses the customer will incur", so the line must carry the running cost the runbook already states ("plus about $12–40 to run your first weekend"); (2) it is only true if the buyer *owns* the instance | resolve the ownership question first (see §4); if a royalty exists, "own the brand and the domain; tjlabs keeps X% for Y months" is the truthful form |
| "Your own employee and employer" | **clear** | sets a work expectation; no result implied | lead with it on the $100 and $200 doors; it is the honest answer to "is this passive?" |
| "Level up" | **clear** | game/identity framing, no result | never "level up your income"; fine as "level up from a kit to a business" between tiers, which also sells the ladder |
| "We did most of the work for you!" | **clear if substantiated** | a product-completeness claim, substantiated by the asset list and week-1 calendar; a third-party completeness audit (ADVERTISING_GENERAL §6) makes it bulletproof | keep "most", never "all" or "done for you" (DFY is the scam market's word); list what is left for the buyer to do right under it |
| "Everything you need to start" | **clear if literally true per tier** | X "non-existent features" clause bites if the door promises a thing the pack lacks | the asset list on the door is the substantiation |
| "Start Saturday" | **clear** | a time budget, not a result | fine at every tier where the week-1 calendar exists |
| "Make $X / earn back the price / pays for itself" | **prohibited** | earnings claim under 437; "results within a specific period" under X; "income promises" under TikTok | never; the price sheet is the only dollar figure allowed |

## 4. The one collision: "become a business owner" versus "tjlabs takes a percentage and partial ownership"

The owner gave both directions within fifteen minutes (hub 01:27; in-session ~01:40). They are compatible only in one shape. "Become a business owner" and "nobody else gets this one" are true if the buyer owns the instance outright and tjlabs is paid a **royalty or deferred price** (a licence-shaped percentage, LAW_AND_POLICY_FLAGS §8f(i)). They are **not** true if tjlabs holds **partial ownership**; the door would then have to say "co-own a business with tjlabs", which breaks the angle at exactly the $1,000 tier where the angle is strongest and paid X is viable. Research finding, not a ruling: the "own it, we keep a percentage for a term" shape is the only one under which the owner's creative direction and the owner's revenue direction are both truthful on the same door.

## 5. Evidence behind the policy calls above

- **The FTC case that defines the line.** Arise Virtual Solutions pitched "run your own company", "be your own boss", "kiss your 9-to-5 goodbye" alongside "up to $18/hour" when the platform average was ~$12 and 99.9% of participants earned less than $18; the FTC's July 2024 settlement was $7 million and ~$6.7 million went back to consumers in August 2025 ([FTC business blog 2024-07](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2024/07/gig-work-company-pay-7-million-settle-ftc-charges-involving-deceptive-earnings-claims-business)). The identity language was not the violation; the paired earnings figure was. That is the whole reason the companion lines above carry prices and running costs and never income.
- **Implied earnings claims count.** FTC guidance for business-opportunity sellers: a claim "expressly or by implication" about how much a person can earn must be put in writing, and the Disclosure Document has a checkbox for whether you "stated – or implied" earnings, with an Earnings Claim Statement attached if so ([FTC guidance: selling a work-at-home or other business opportunity](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/selling-work-home-or-other-business-opportunity-revised-rule-may-apply-you-1)). "Pays for itself", "earn it back the first weekend", and a third-party valuation on the door are all implied earnings claims.
- **Generic "be your own boss" underperforms on its own in the franchise market.** Franchise-recruitment practitioners report that serious candidates treat "be your own boss" and "join a growing brand" as vague and want the model, expectations, investment, training, support, and a realistic path to opening; lead quality beats lead volume; region-specific ads improve lead quality ~38%; a 5-minute response converts ~9x better; 87% of franchisors use Google paid (including YouTube) for recruitment ([operandio](https://operandio.com/franchise-recruitment/), [franfunnel](https://www.franfunnel.com/blog/franchise-lead-generation-strategies), [joinvalley](https://www.joinvalley.co/blog/top-10-franchise-lead-generation-strategies/blog-goatfish)) `(secondary)`. Translation for our doors: the identity line opens, the concrete companion (asset list, running cost, week-1 calendar, support boundary) is what converts, and for the $10,000 tier a fast human reply after the lead form is worth more than another creative.

## 6. Format notes for the angle

- Vertical video: open on the buyer's hands (cards, laptop, signs), not on tjlabs; the line "your own employee and employer" as the first caption; the price sheet as a price sheet; the week-1 calendar as the closing frame.
- Carousel: card 1 identity line ("Be your own boss on Saturdays"), card 2 what's inside (asset list), card 3 the price and the running cost, card 4 the door.
- Founder-led for $10,000: Bryce says "we did most of the work" and then shows the work.
- Every platform's review reads the landing page: the door must carry the same price, running cost, asset list, and refund policy the ad implies.

## 7. A classier angle for the higher-ticket tiers — $1,000 PLANT and $10,000 HEAVY (owner direction, ~01:45 EDT: "then a classier angle for higher ticket products")

The consumer angle (§1–3) shouts; this one states. The buyers at these tiers (Renee/Kevin, 32–52, household income $100k+, corporate refugee; Owen, 35–60, owner-operator) have sat through franchise discovery days and read BizBuySell listings. They trust documents, not energy. The register is acquisition, not hustle.

**Principles**
1. **Understatement.** No exclamation marks, no emoji, no countdowns, no "limited". One price, stated once.
2. **Evidence before aspiration.** Lead with what exists: the brand, the domain, the door, the costed inventory list, the supplier list, the 90-day operating calendar, the support boundary in writing, the third-party completeness audit when it exists (ADVERTISING_GENERAL §6). This is the acquisition vocabulary the buyer already speaks: documented, complete, verified, handed over.
3. **Ownership stated plainly.** "Yours outright. No royalties." Or, if the owner keeps a royalty, the terms in one sentence. At this tier transparency reads as premium; vagueness reads as franchise.
4. **Work acknowledged, not hidden.** The classy form of "your own employee and employer" is: *"We built it and documented it. Operating it is yours."*
5. **Only true scarcity.** One instance per buyer is real and is the only scarcity the factory law allows: *"One owner. This instance is not sold again."*
6. **The comparison, once, sourced, without adjectives.** *"A franchise fee in this category starts at $10,350, before royalties."*
7. **Invite diligence.** *"Ask for the inventory list before you ask about the price."* It pre-qualifies the buyer and flatters the competence the corporate refugee is buying back.

**Vocabulary swap (consumer angle → higher-ticket angle)**

| consumer tiers | $1,000 / $10,000 |
|---|---|
| side hustle, kit, pack (headline) | a business you operate; a complete, documented business (keep "pack" in the SKU, not the headline) |
| level up | step into ownership |
| we did most of the work for you! | built and documented; the operating is yours |
| start Saturday | a 90-day operating calendar is included (a plan, not a result) |
| cheap, cash-now, no app | the full inventory, costed; the supplier list; the support boundary |
| your own boss | owner-operator; one owner per instance |
| grow, scale, crush it | operate |

**Words to avoid at these tiers, beyond the global list.** *Investment, asset, returns, appreciate, wealth, portfolio* — X's Financial Services policy treats "investment opportunities, and offers to increase wealth" as a restricted category needing certification, and the same words pull the pack toward securities and business-opportunity regimes. *Franchise* (Franchise Rule vocabulary). *Exclusive territory* (a franchise-style control promise). *Passive, turnkey income, proven system.* Prices and inclusions only.

**Candidate lines — $1,000 PLANT (Renee / Kevin)**
- "A business, built and documented. Yours outright. → $1,000. Brand, domain, booking door, costed inventory list, supplier list, 90-day operating calendar. No royalties. One owner per instance."
- "Not a course. Not a template. A named business with its own door."
- "The work of starting has been done. The work of owning begins."
- "Independently audited for completeness." *(only once a rating partner has actually audited it)*
- "A franchise fee in this category starts at $10,350, before royalties. This is $1,000, and it is yours."

**Candidate lines — $10,000 HEAVY (Owen)**
- "A business unit, delivered. → $10,000. Brand, site or app, booking and checkout door, equipment list, hiring and insurance checklists, 90-day plan, support boundary in writing. No royalties."
- "Built, documented, and handed over. We remain responsible for exactly what the support boundary says."
- "For operators adding a line, not for people looking for one."
- "Ask for the inventory list before you ask about the price."
- Founder letter, first person, three short paragraphs: what was built, what is included, what is not. Signed. No call to action beyond a reply.

**If a royalty exists** (the owner's 01:27 direction), the classy form is the plain one: *"You own the brand, the domain, and the door. tjlabs retains X% of gross for Y months and remains responsible for updates for that term."* Stated terms read as premium. Partial ownership cannot be made classy at this tier; it reads as a franchise without the disclosure document (see §4 and LAW_AND_POLICY_FLAGS §8).

**Format**
- Door as a prospectus: a table of inclusions, a table of exclusions ("what you do not get"), the audit seal and report link, the support boundary, the refund policy, the price once. Serif headline, whitespace, one image (the instance's own door), no stock photos of people smiling at laptops.
- Ads: for X, a text-first founder post promoted as-is (the native register; build-in-public beats produced creative for B2B on X); for Google Search intent ("buy a business", "franchise under 10k", "add a service line"), a plain headline with sitelinks to the inventory list, the support boundary, and the audit; for video, fifteen seconds of the documents themselves scrolling with a founder voice, no music drop.
- Response time is part of the product at $10,000: franchise-recruitment data puts a five-minute reply at ~9x conversion versus slow follow-up (§5). The lead form should hand off to a human the same hour.
