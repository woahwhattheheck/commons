# RFQ 18649 engagement mobilization kit

**Status: reusable preparation template. No engagement, staffing assignment, appointment, or start date is confirmed by this kit.**

Use this kit to organize an authorized assessment from kickoff through final written report. Dates remain relative to an eventual agreed kickoff. The eight-week plan follows the RFQ's published phase windows; the six-week option is a proposed compressed design that needs explicit agreement on staffing, evidence availability, and review capacity.

The controlling RFQ and written amendments govern. The proposed TJLabs workshare is a separate, unaccepted commercial arrangement. Its technical-artifact milestones do not replace the supplier's University-facing report obligations. [R1; R8; E1]

## 1. How to use the kit

The [editable coordination forms](COORDINATION_FORMS.md) provide the contact directory, participant roster, grouped-session sheet, and decision/action continuation fields. The companion XLSX uses condensed registers with separate IDs: W01-W09 and D001-D008. This guide's detailed W1-01-W1-13 and M01-M11 registers remain distinct; choose one operational register for each workflow and retain references when carrying entries between them.

1. Before receiving University documents or access, establish the executed NDA and the authorized confidential workspace. Use public and synthetic preparation material until those conditions are met. [R10]
2. At contracting, confirm the actual kickoff date and integrated schedule. Select the eight-week plan, or negotiate the six-week compression only after its prerequisites are demonstrably available. [R8; D1]
3. Assign people to the role slots below, agree the interview sample and review arrangements, and record decisions in the decision register. Role slots describe work; they do not appoint anyone. [R3; R7; D1]
4. Run the proposed kickoff agenda, update the first-week checklist with evidence of completion, and carry unresolved dependencies into weekly status. [R2; R7; D1]
5. Maintain source-to-finding traceability, capture disagreements, and share the final report with the participating groups through the agreed confidential route. [R4; R6]

**Source labels:** R1-R11 identify RFQ requirements in section 8; E1 identifies the current proposed workshare exhibit. D1 means a newly designed operating method in this kit, not a requirement quoted from the University.

## 2. Proposed kickoff agenda

**Format:** 60-minute working agenda, designed for the supplier engagement lead, AIS day-to-day point of contact, participating group leads, and sponsor participation appropriate to scope decisions. Actual attendees, format, location, and date must be agreed separately. The RFQ expects kickoff onsite when reasonable; this template neither reserves travel nor commits an individual. [R7; R8; D1]

| Elapsed minutes | Working discussion | Decision or tangible output | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-5 | Confirm the constructive assessment purpose, what leadership needs to decide, and the role of strengths as well as gaps. | A short success statement and named role holders in the private engagement record. | R1, R7; D1 timing |
| 5-15 | Walk through ESS, RIS, and IAM against software development, security, deployment, and AI readiness. Confirm exclusions and relevant service context. | A 12-cell coverage frame; scope assumptions and exclusions recorded without invented ratings. | R1, R9 |
| 15-25 | Review evidence custody, NDA status, initial artifacts, source ownership, access restrictions, and the private route for concerning findings. | Evidence request list; custody and escalation decisions; outstanding access dependencies. | R4, R6, R10 |
| 25-35 | Plan the interview sample across leaders, managers, and individual contributors; identify where follow-up examples are needed. | Role-based candidate sample of approximately 6-8 people per group; coverage rationale and availability still to be confirmed. | R3 |
| 35-45 | Explain maturity anchors, evidence confidence, current-state characterization, and peer fit. Examine how different stacks can achieve comparable outcomes. | Agreed methodology questions, unresolved calibration choices, and peer-selection rationale. | R4, R5 |
| 45-55 | Review the relative delivery calendar, staffing capacity, weekly status, draft review participants, and report distribution. | Preferred schedule scenario; dependencies; review windows; weekly status format. No individual appointment created. | R2, R7, R8; D1 |
| 55-60 | Read back decisions, unresolved items, owners by role, and next evidence needed. | Updated decision register and first-week checklist with explicit blocked items. | D1 |

**Suggested opening:** "The purpose is to understand current practices across the three groups, validate strengths, and identify achievable improvements. We will substantiate conclusions with examples and preserve gaps or disagreements when the evidence is incomplete. Today we need a shared scope, a workable evidence and interview plan, and a delivery sequence everyone can support." [R1, R4; D1 wording]

### Initial evidence request structure

Use one row per source or source family: **request ID; group(s); assessment area(s); practice/question supported; artifact description; custodian role; access restriction; requested version/period; source location; received date; excerpt/page locator; missing or conflicting evidence; follow-up owner; status.** This schema is a proposed operating design. One document can support several cells without becoming several independent observations. [R3, R4; D1]

Include the following source families for ESS, RIS, and IAM, proportionate to what exists and is authorized: team/organization context; architecture and stack context; SDLC/runbook/security/deployment documents; concrete request-to-production examples; recent-project examples; and postmortems. The RFQ calls for additional corroborating examples as findings develop, so the initial request is not a fixed ceiling on evidence. Recent projects supply context; they do not authorize a deep dive into an individual application or service. [R3, R4, R9]

## 4. Relative delivery calendars

### Eight-week plan aligned to the RFQ phase windows

The RFQ describes kickoff/discovery in Week 1, interviews/artifact review in Weeks 2-4, analysis/synthesis in Weeks 4-5, draft/review in Weeks 6-7, and final report in Week 7-8. The finer-grained outputs below are proposed operating checkpoints. Weekly status runs throughout the engagement. [R2, R7, R8; D1]

| Week | Work and overlap | Reviewable output / dependency |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kickoff and discovery; establish scope, custody, roles, interview sample, and evidence requests. | Mobilization packet and open-dependency register. NDA must precede confidential intake. |
| 2 | Interviews and artifact review across the groups; begin source-linked observations and follow-up requests. | Evidence register, interview coverage, and unanswered questions. |
| 3 | Continue interviews/artifact review; substantiate practices with concrete examples and preserve differing accounts. | Updated coverage and corroboration records; early themes remain provisional. |
| 4 | Finish planned interview/artifact review while analysis begins; identify follow-up evidence needed for group and cross-group findings. | Per-group draft observations and cross-cutting hypotheses linked to sources; unresolved gaps remain visible. |
| 5 | Analyze and synthesize; calibrate the maturity characterization and peer comparisons; develop practical recommendations and phased effort estimates. | Supported findings and draft roadmap inputs, including strengths, gaps, limits, and dependencies. |
| 6 | Assemble and share the draft report with the AIS day-to-day point of contact and sponsor for the agreed review. | Draft executive summary, method/peer approach, cross-cutting themes, and recommendations; review register opened. |
| 7 | Incorporate feedback and reconcile evidence, interpretation, and wording changes; prepare the final report. Delivery may fall in this week if the agreed process is complete. | Dispositioned comments and a coherent final candidate; substantive disagreements and limits remain explicit. |
| 8 | Complete final written report delivery and agreed confidential distribution; record acceptance through the designated role and activate closeout tracking. | Final report and delivery record. Acceptance is recorded only when actually given; closeout due date is derived from actual engagement completion. |

### Six-week compressed option: proposed design

This variant preserves the five engagement phases but overlaps production more tightly than the RFQ's illustrative Week 1-8 table. Use it only when the supplier and AIS explicitly agree the revised sequence and there is enough staffed capacity, timely evidence access, representative interview availability, and a substantive review window. A target of six weeks does not justify reducing the three-group/four-area scope or replacing missing evidence with assumptions. [R1, R2, R4, R8; D1]

| Week | Compressed sequence | Condition / output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Complete kickoff/discovery; establish custody, roles, interview sample, method, and initial evidence intake. | NDA and access arrangements complete; evidence and interview availability support parallel work. |
| 2 | Conduct interviews and artifact review across all groups; document sources and request corroboration as work proceeds. | Sufficient staffing and group availability; no claim that 6-8 people equals 6-8 separate meetings. |
| 3 | Complete planned primary interviews/review while analysis and cross-group synthesis begin; pursue targeted follow-ups. | Coverage and contradictory evidence checked before conclusions stabilize. |
| 4 | Complete analysis, benchmarking, recommendation prioritization, and draft assembly; share the draft for review. | Full report structure, explicit evidence limitations, and a review window agreed before adopting this variant. |
| 5 | Conduct sponsor/day-to-day review, incorporate feedback, and resolve material evidence issues where possible. | Consolidated disposition record; unresolved matters retained with their implications. |
| 6 | Complete final report and confidential distribution; record actual acceptance and closeout responsibilities. | Final delivery plus completion record; return/destruction and written confirmation remain due within 30 days of completion. |

**If compression fails:** identify the affected evidence, staffing, or review dependency; record its effect on quality and timing; have the accountable supplier and AIS roles agree the revised schedule. Continue independent supported work. The proposed subcontract's evidence dependencies do not change its payment triggers. [E1; D1]

**Assessment calendar versus improvement roadmap:** the six/eight-week calendar ends with the assessment report. Recommendations are separately phased into 0-90, 90-180, and 180+ days, with effort and skill/training/tooling dependencies; this kit does not promise implementation within the assessment. [R6]

**Optional readout:** RFQ follow-on discussion/readout sessions are separately priced, outside the base engagement, and may be selected after final report delivery. Do not insert an automatic readout obligation into either base calendar. If selected, separately agree scope, audience, timing, and whether onsite delivery is reasonable. [R6, R8; E1]

## 5. Roles and decision ownership

These are RFQ role definitions and proposed delivery role slots. The private engagement record must identify actual people after authorized appointment. No contact details or personal availability belong in this public template.

| Role slot | Responsibility and decision boundary | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| AIS engagement sponsor | Executive accountability, scope confirmation, and final report acceptance. | R7 |
| AIS day-to-day point of contact | Engagement management; coordinates artifacts and interviews; primary supplier interface. | R7 |
| ESS / RIS / IAM group leads | Coordinate group interview arrangements and artifact sharing; help establish representative role coverage. | R3, R7 |
| Supplier engagement lead | Accountable supplier contact for delivery quality, timeline, integrated schedule, and appropriate escalation. | R7 |
| Supplier methodology/analysis lead | Proposed working role for transparent maturity anchors, peer-fit rationale, evidence analysis, and cross-group synthesis; final accountability stays with the engagement lead. | R4, R5; D1 |
| Technical production role | Proposed working role for evidence organization, source links, matrix/report inputs, and controlled revisions. Any TJLabs scope must follow the separately agreed workshare. | E1; D1 |

**Proposed workshare boundary:** TJLabs' current exhibit assigns technical evidence organization, traceable matrix/findings/roadmap inputs, and consistency/currentness material to its proposed workshare. The prospective prime retains the University relationship, integrated schedule, interviews/staffing/onsite commitments, professional judgment, and final report/readout authority. Its proposed payment triggers remain 40% on written authorization/kickoff, 40% on draft-package delivery, and 20% on final-package acceptance. Input dependencies affect work sequencing; they do not redefine these triggers. [E1]

## 7. Boundary cases for using the kit correctly

These are synthetic desk-review cases demonstrating the intended operating method. They are not observations about AIS, measured delivery outcomes, or proof of stakeholder acceptance.

| Case | Expected handling | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The initial evidence folder is ready, but the NDA is not executed. | Keep University documentation and system intake blocked. Continue public/synthetic methodology and planning work; do not treat a kickoff target as permission to receive it. | R10 |
| A manager describes an established release practice, while supplied examples contradict the account. | Preserve both accounts, request clarifying evidence, and keep the characterization appropriately qualified. An interview claim alone does not establish the practice. | R4 |
| Week 3 interview availability falls short of the compressed plan. | Record coverage and schedule impacts; agree a revised sequence or timeline instead of claiming missing coverage is complete. | R3, R8; D1 |
| A materially concerning operational issue is identified. | Use the agreed private route to both day-to-day contact and sponsor before report publication; retain the finding in the final report. | R6 |
| An exposed secret appears in an authorized evidence item. | Handle it confidentially and notify the day-to-day point of contact within 24 hours through the agreed responsible-disclosure procedure; do not copy secret values into shared planning material. | R10 |
| A requester asks the team to recommend a specific commercial vendor or perform an application deep dive. | Keep the request outside the current scope. Under the proposed E1 workshare boundary, the product/vendor exclusion can be reopened only by a formal amendment to the controlling solicitation and fresh scope review. RFQ attribute 7 separately requires the University to communicate scope changes in writing. | R9, R11; E1 |
| The report is final, but no optional readout has been selected. | Deliver the base written report and its agreed distribution. Keep optional discussion sessions outside the base schedule and uncommitted. | R6 |
| A final report contains a remaining evidence gap or disagreement. | State the limit and its effect; do not erase it to make the deliverable look complete. Record actual review/acceptance separately from technical completion. | R4; E1 |
