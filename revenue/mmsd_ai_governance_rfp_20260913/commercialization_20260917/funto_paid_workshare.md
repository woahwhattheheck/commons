## Proposed TJLabs workstream

### 1. AI inventory and evidence architecture

Deliver a practical schema and review method for up to **30 identified AI use cases/systems** supplied by the prime/MMSD, covering:

- owner and business process;
- GenAI / predictive / embedded / operational / agentic category;
- data class and external-provider handling;
- action/autonomy surface;
- public-record and retention status;
- model/vendor identity and material-change evidence;
- human override, manual fallback, monitoring, incident plan, and validation evidence.

The existing buyer-neutral MMSD engine remains relative to supplied rows and does not claim enterprise-inventory completeness.

### 2. Operational + agentic risk tier design

Produce the technical criteria that keep administrative GenAI distinct from AI that can influence treatment, infrastructure, or enterprise actions:

- information/data risk;
- operational/physical consequence;
- autonomy/tool authority;
- recoverability and manual fallback;
- monitoring/validation;
- vendor/model drift;
- records/evidence preservation.

### 3. Vendor AI / data-sovereignty control set

Produce a reusable vendor questionnaire/control matrix for:

- retention and training use;
- telemetry/support access;
- subprocessors;
- residency;
- deletion/export;
- security/incident notification;
- model or feature changes;
- audit/evidence availability;
- termination/exit;
- integration privileges and human-approval boundaries.

### 4. Action-authority and incident controls

Define control language and implementation patterns that distinguish:

- suggest;
- draft/prepare;
- execute under bounded delegated authority;
- nondelegable / named-human approval.

Add AI incident evidence and rollback/fallback requirements for data leakage, unauthorized action, model drift/change, operational anomaly, and unsafe or unreviewed outputs.

### 5. Scenario validation

Run **six structured technical table-top scenarios** across administrative and operational AI. Each scenario produces:

- system/use-case facts;
- proposed tier;
- required controls;
- human decision point;
- evidence/record requirement;
- incident/rollback route where relevant;
- unresolved owner/counsel decisions.

### 6. Technical policy traceability

Review the prime's draft technical sections once and return a control-traceability matrix mapping the policy to the inventory/risk/vendor/incident artifacts.
