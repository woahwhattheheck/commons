## Rules for using the cohort

1. **Define the unit before comparing it.** Record campus coverage, central versus distributed functions, research infrastructure, medical/public-safety responsibilities, and shared-service obligations. Compare the relevant service slice where whole organizations differ.
2. **Obtain a common staffing measure.** Ask for an as-of date, employee headcount and FTE separately, vacancies, students, contractors, and included shared or embedded teams. Budget authorization measures available positions; it does not prove occupied capacity. An undated page is not a 2026 observation merely because it was retrieved in 2026.
3. **Separate descriptions from performance evidence.** Organization pages describe responsibilities. Budgets authorize resources. Annual reports provide self-reported implementation examples. None alone establishes control effectiveness or a causal efficiency improvement.
4. **Use practice-specific comparisons.** Ask how ownership is assigned, how cross-service changes are coordinated, and what evidence demonstrates an outcome. Do not transfer a vendor choice, ticket volume, maturity score or staff-per-user ratio without matching definitions and periods.
5. **Keep the selection provisional.** UNKNOWN means no usable value was established in the sources read; it does not mean the organization has no record. Confirm the proposed cohort and scope with the assessment team before treating it as a matched sample. No contact has been made.
